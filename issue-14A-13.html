Session timing and utility coalitions

A slow January, real talks in March and April, Senate first, and companies agree before approaching Senator Elman.

More on Legislative strategy

What was said

Only one company has committed so far. Reviewing the October 13 meetings, Speaker 1 said only American Water committed to support an unspecified initiative, and the electric generation companies have separate initiatives that may not be taken up in the spring session.

Agree first, then go to the Senate. Speaker 1 advised getting the companies together on what they can agree to with Senator Elman, the sooner the better, and taking a Senate first path because of gridlock in the House.

Expect a slow start. Speaker 1 does not see much happening in January. Around March people wake up as deadlines and extensions arrive, and by April the calendar pushes.

Keep the process tight. Keep the meddlers out and keep communication disciplined among the designated principals. Last year's effort, referred to as 40-83, achieved little because efforts were fragmented.

Why it matters

My reading

Timing and discipline decide whether a coalition has a seat when the bill language is drafted. The calendar means the real window to agree is the fall and winter, before March.

Why early agreement matters

Once a sponsor and a staffer start drafting, the language hardens quickly. A group that arrives with an agreed ask can shape it. A group that arrives late can only react.

Generation fleet transition

Storage, combined cycle, and gas plant ownership changes shape IRP input. A combined cycle working group is planned.

More on Plans and regulation

What was said

Storage and carbon shifting are under discussion. The group talked about storage purchases and emission shifting under CEJA and state law.

Generation bills are being shaped. Working groups are developing bills for PJM and MISO, emphasizing the local and grid effects of early thermal closures.

Combined cycle gas faces lower pressure. These units face less direct pressure before 2040, but operators are organizing to give IRP input. Cordova and J-Power were named, with J-Power's controlling interest said to have been sold to a sovereign fund while J-Power keeps operating.

Simple cycle units in Northern Illinois changed hands. A power cooperative named Dairyland acquired units at the Elwood facility.

The decision was to form a group. A dedicated working group of combined cycle operators will prepare IRP testimony.

Why it matters

My reading

Ownership changes can change who spends money on environmental upgrades and who answers a call for help. A united group of operators can bring one clear message into the IRP process.

Say it in plain words

A working group of combined cycle gas plant owners is planned so they can give joint input to the resource plan. These plants face less pressure before 2040. A sovereign fund is said to have bought a controlling interest in one plant, and a cooperative acquired units at Elwood. Ownership shifts like these change long term commitments, so each name and fact needs checking.

Combined cycle versus simple cycle

A combined cycle plant uses waste heat to make extra power, so it is more efficient and runs more hours. A simple cycle unit is cheaper to build and is often used for peaks. They face different rules and different markets.

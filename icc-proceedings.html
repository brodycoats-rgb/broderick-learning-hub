Illinois Energy Regulatory Strategy Briefing

Grid Planning • Resource Adequacy • Future of Gas • Large Loads • Clean Energy Implementation

Executive Positioning

Illinois is entering a defining energy-regulatory cycle. Investment requirements, reliability risks, decarbonization mandates, data-center growth, affordability pressures, and utility cost recovery are no longer separate issues — they now move together. This briefing reframes those proceedings as a single strategic landscape for Government Relations: where decisions are being made, what narratives are forming, and where leadership engagement can shape outcomes.

The report is designed for fast executive use. The front section provides the message architecture and leadership implications. The detailed body preserves the docket-by-docket record, timelines, risks, and source material needed for follow-up, testimony preparation, stakeholder engagement, and legislative strategy.

30-Second Executive Summary

Illinois energy policy is now an implementation challenge, with reliability, affordability, decarbonization, large-load growth, and cost recovery all moving together.

The biggest near-term risks are capacity shortfalls, grid investment approvals, data-center cost allocation, the Future of Gas proceeding, and execution of CRGA requirements.

Government Relations should treat these dockets as connected policy signals, not separate regulatory silos, because each one can quickly become a legislative, stakeholder, or customer-affordability issue.

The core message is simple: invest in the grid, recover costs fairly, protect customers, and maintain reliability while Illinois transitions to a cleaner energy future.

Executive Plain Language Script

Illinois is entering a very important energy cycle. The state is trying to modernize the grid, add cleaner resources, manage data-center growth, address the future role of gas, and keep customer bills affordable, all at the same time. These are not separate issues anymore. A decision in one proceeding can affect reliability, customer costs, economic development, and the political narrative around energy policy.

The main thing to understand is that Illinois has moved from setting clean-energy goals to figuring out how to actually deliver them. That means hard choices about what gets built, who pays, how quickly projects move, and how we protect customers while still maintaining a reliable system. For Government Relations, the job is to stay ahead of where these regulatory decisions could become legislative debates, stakeholder concerns, or affordability challenges.

The strongest framing is this: we need to invest in reliability and modernization, recover costs in a fair and transparent way, make sure large new loads pay their share, and keep affordability at the center of the conversation.

Strategic Narrative

Invest: Utilities must invest in grid modernization, reliability, interconnection, storage enablement, and resilience.

Recover: Those investments must be recovered through rates, tariffs, riders, procurement charges, or other approved mechanisms.

Earn: The allowed return, performance metrics, and prudence review determine whether utilities can earn on those investments.

Reinvest: Approved recovery becomes the foundation for the next cycle of planning, capital deployment, reliability commitments, and policy implementation.

Closing Perspective

Executive Summary

Illinois is in the middle of its most consequential energy-regulatory cycle in a decade. Three forces are converging at once: a surge in electricity demand (driven heavily by data centers), the retirement of roughly 3 GW of fossil generation by 2030, and a sweeping new law — the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act (CRGA / Public Act 104-0458), effective June 1, 2026 — that layers new planning obligations on top of the existing CEJA framework.

This briefing provides a section-by-section workup of each proceeding we track: what it is, why it matters, the legal basis, the goals, the key dates, who the players are, and where the risks and openings sit. It closes with a “What You May Be Missing” section covering cross-cutting issues — the Resource Adequacy Study, PJM/MISO capacity markets, energy storage procurement, the nuclear moratorium repeal, Virtual Power Plants, beneficial electrification, and interconnection/siting reform — that shape every docket below.

Five Executive Watchpoints

Grid Plans (2028–2031): Ameren filed Jan 20, 2026 (Docket 26-0051); ComEd filed in parallel. ICC final orders expected ~December 2026 after an ~11-month statutory review.

First statewide IRP: ICC-led initial report is due November 16, 2026 — a hard statutory deadline and a genuinely new planning instrument for Illinois.

Future of Gas: Phase 2C workshops run through September 24, 2026, even as an industry coalition petitions (Docket 26-0146) to terminate the proceeding.

Large Load / data centers: ComEd’s LDPAC tariff was approved March 19, 2026; a follow-up investigation (Docket 26-0364) on ratepayer protections and cost recovery is underway.

Storage & capacity: The IPA’s first energy-storage procurement (~1,038 MW toward a 3,000 MW target) lands by August 26, 2026, against a backdrop of PJM capacity shortfalls and record prices.

How the Pieces Fit Together

These proceedings are not silos — they feed each other. The Resource Adequacy Study identifies shortfalls; the IRP builds a statewide roadmap to close them; the Grid Plans fund the distribution build-out; storage and renewable procurements add supply; the Future of Gas process decides the gas system’s long-term role; and the Large Load tariffs govern how the biggest new demand connects and pays. CRGA is the statute stitching them together.

1. Grid Plan Process (Multi-Year Integrated Grid Plans)

What it is

Utilities' 4-year distribution investment & rate roadmaps (“MYIGP”), reviewed and approved by the ICC.

Legal basis

CEJA (PA 102-0662), 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17; refined by CRGA (PA 104-0458).

Who

ComEd and Ameren Illinois file; ICC decides; AG, IIEC, environmental & consumer intervenors participate.

Current cycle

2028–2031 plans — Ameren Docket 26-0051 (filed Jan 20, 2026); ComEd parallel filing.

Key deadline

~11-month statutory review → ICC final orders expected ~December 2026.

Purpose & Goals

The Grid Plan process replaced the old “formula rate” system with forward-looking, performance-oriented planning. Utilities must show that proposed capital spending advances CEJA’s decarbonization and equity goals — not just reliability — and tie investment to measurable performance metrics.

Modernize and harden the distribution grid (stronger poles/wires, automation, preventive maintenance) to cut outage frequency and duration.

Enable the clean-energy transition: faster DER/solar/storage interconnection and two-way power flow on the distribution system.

Direct a substantial share of investment to equity-eligible communities.

Contain cost growth and align spending with approved performance metrics and return-on-equity (ROE) determinations.

What to Watch / Risks

Spending haircuts: The ICC cut both prior plans sharply (esp. Ameren ~75%). Expect the AG and IIEC to again push reductions; the size of the approved capital envelope is the central fight.

ROE levels: Prior orders set ROE at 8.905% (ComEd) and 8.715% (Ameren) — below utility asks. ROE will again drive the revenue requirement.

CRGA overlay: The 2028–2031 plans are the first filed under both CEJA and CRGA; expect new expectations on load growth, storage enablement, and interconnection speed.

Appeals: The first-cycle orders drew appellate action; legal challenges can reshape the framework mid-stream.

2. Integrated Resource Plan (IRP)

What it is

Illinois's first statewide IRP — a comprehensive study of whether supply meets demand over 5/10/15/20-year horizons, plus solution sets to close gaps.

Legal basis

CRGA (PA 104-0458); IPA Act 20 ILCS 3855.

Who

ICC leads, in coordination with IPA, IEPA, and the Illinois Finance Authority (IFA).

Deliverable

Initial IRP report due November 16, 2026; updated every 4 years thereafter.

Coordination

Merges with the Resource Adequacy Study & RA Mitigation Plan workshops.

Purpose & Goals

The IRP is a genuinely new instrument in Illinois. Where the Grid Plan covers distribution investment, the IRP takes a bird’s-eye view of the entire resource picture — generation, storage, demand-side, and reliability — and produces a roadmap the state can act on. It is the analytic backbone that ties reliability warnings to concrete procurement and policy responses.

Evaluate future electricity resource needs over 5-, 10-, 15-, and 20-year timelines.

Forecast peak demand and energy usage, including large-load / data-center growth.

Analyze all generation and resource options against a range of load forecasts.

Model emissions, affordability, and reliability trade-offs to guide state decisions.

Related: IPA Long-Term Renewable Resources Procurement Plan

Distinct from the IRP, the IPA’s Long-Term Plan governs REC procurement and the Illinois Shines / Solar for All programs. The 2026 Plan (Docket 25-0945) was approved by the ICC on February 17, 2026, finalized April 20, 2026, and modified June 1, 2026 to conform to CRGA. What to Watch / Risks

Hard deadline: November 16, 2026 is statutory. Slippage or a thin first report would be politically notable.

Shortfall findings: The IRP will formalize the capacity-shortfall warnings (2029 north / 2031 downstate) and shape the state’s procurement response.

Inter-agency coordination: Four agencies plus the RA Study must align on inputs — a genuine execution risk on a compressed timeline.

3. Future of Gas

What it is

An ICC investigation (own motion) into how Illinois's gas distribution system adapts to state decarbonization goals.

Docket

24-0158 (opened Mar 7, 2024); facilitated by Celia Johnson Consulting.

Legal basis

CEJA (PA 102-0662) decarbonization objectives.

Scale

800+ participants; 100+ pilot ideas collected; 79 legislative/regulatory policy options under review.

Key window

Phase 2C workshops run through September 24, 2026.

Purpose & Goals

The Future of Gas proceeding is where Illinois confronts a hard question: as the state decarbonizes, what happens to the gas distribution system, the utilities that own it, and the customers who depend on it? It is exploratory and consensus-building rather than a rate case — but its recommendations will shape legislation and future dockets.

Identify the decarbonization issues facing the gas system (safety, affordability, stranded-asset risk, equity).

Educate stakeholders on decarbonization pathways and technologies (electrification, RNG, hydrogen, geothermal networks).

Test approaches through pilots and comparative analysis.

Develop legislative and regulatory policy options for the Commission and General Assembly.

4. Large Load Tariffs & Data Centers

What it is

Tariffs and rules governing how very large loads (50 MW+ “LDPACs,” chiefly data centers) interconnect, post security, and pay — protecting other ratepayers from cost shifts.

Dockets

ComEd tariff 25-0677/25-0679 (Final Order Mar 19, 2026); follow-up investigation 26-0364.

Legal basis

Public Utilities Act; ICC ratemaking authority.

The pressure

ComEd pipeline of 75+ projects (~28,000 MW) — about 1.2× its all-time 24,000 MW peak.

Next step

ICC follow-up investigation (~8 months) on ratepayer protections & cost recovery.

Purpose & Goals

Data-center demand is the single biggest new variable on the Illinois grid. Northern Illinois (ComEd/PJM) faces potential capacity shortfalls as early as 2029. The large-load tariff framework is the state’s tool to let this growth connect without saddling ordinary customers with the cost or the reliability risk.

Ensure large loads pay their fair share of interconnection and system costs (security deposits, letters of credit).

Protect residential and small-business ratepayers from cost-shifting and stranded infrastructure.

Manage reliability as concentrated, continuous loads connect faster than new generation is built.

Coordinate with PJM/MISO capacity and “bring-your-own-generation” dynamics.

6. Strategic Blind Spots & Cross-Cutting Issues

These issues do not always appear as clean standalone dockets, but they shape the policy environment, public narrative, and cost-recovery risk around every proceeding. They should be treated as strategic signals — not background noise.

A. Resource Adequacy Study & RA Mitigation Plan

On December 15, 2025, the IPA, IEPA, and ICC jointly released the 2025 Resource Adequacy Study, projecting capacity shortfalls as early as 2029 in Northern Illinois (ComEd/PJM) and 2031 downstate (Ameren/MISO), driven largely by data-center load. This study is the factual trigger for the IRP, storage procurement, and large-load rules — it’s the “why now” behind everything.

B. PJM & MISO Capacity Markets (the federal overlay)

Much of what drives Illinois bills is decided at the RTO/FERC level, not the ICC. PJM’s capacity auction cleared at record highs and still fell ~6.8 GW short for the 2028 delivery year; PJM projects 30–34 GW of new large-load demand by the early 2030s and up to 70 GW by 2038. PJM has filed a Reliability Backstop Procurement and large-load curtailment proposals at FERC that push cost-allocation decisions onto states. MISO’s 2026/27 capacity prices, by contrast, fell as supply outpaced demand. The ICC dockets increasingly respond to these federal dynamics.

C. Energy Storage Build-Out (3 GW by 2030)

CRGA mandates 3,000 MW of storage by 2030, financed through a bill charge similar to the nuclear-support model, plus a “Storage for All” equity program. Storage is explicitly meant to replace the ~3 GW of fossil generation retiring by 2030 and to firm up wind and solar. Watch the indexed storage credit contract and the initial 1,038 MW procurement.

D. Nuclear Moratorium Repeal

CRGA lifts Illinois’ 40-year ban on new large nuclear reactors (>300 MW) and revises the nuclear-waste fee structure. This won’t produce near-term dockets, but it reshapes the long-run supply picture the IRP must model — and is politically significant.

E. Virtual Power Plants (VPPs)

CRGA creates a VPP program letting customers aggregate batteries, solar, and other behind-the-meter resources to provide grid services and get paid. Regulatory guidance to stand this up is forthcoming — an emerging docket area worth monitoring.

F. Beneficial / Transportation Electrification

Under the EV Act (via CEJA), ComEd and Ameren run Beneficial Electrification Plans (e.g., Ameren’s ChargeSmart; Docket 24-0494 for Plan 2). As EV and fleet electrification grow, these plans intersect with load forecasts and grid planning — a quieter but compounding demand driver.

G. Interconnection & Local Siting Reform

CRGA sets statewide siting standards for storage, wind, and solar; limits local fees; and creates an ICC-administered appeal process for disputes. It also pushes faster interconnection — directly relevant to both grid plans and the large-load queue. Expect implementation dockets and rulemakings.

H. Affordability & Consumer Protection

Ameren all-in residential rates are up roughly 94% since 2021; CUB has warned ComEd bills could rise another 20–25% starting June 2026 as new PJM capacity prices flow through. Affordability is the political lens through which every docket above will be judged — and the CRGA “Storage for All,” EE, and VPP provisions are the state’s medium-term relief levers.

Closing Perspective

Illinois energy policy is moving from aspiration to implementation. The next phase will be decided less by broad statutory goals and more by practical questions of timing, cost recovery, reliability, customer impact, and political durability. The proceedings summarized here are where those choices become real.

For Government Relations, the opportunity is to stay ahead of the convergence: anticipate where regulatory decisions will become legislative issues, where affordability concerns will become public narratives, and where reliability planning will require coordinated engagement across utilities, regulators, policymakers, large customers, and consumer advocates.

What the project is

Ben has a stretch assignment from an executive to build one organized document of the data center issues that could come up in the next legislative session. He brought a rough, AI assisted slide deck with one slide per issue. Each slide shows the existing rules, who wants what, and Ameren Illinois' stance. He is not tied to the format. His draft is due to the team by October 22.

The room liked it. Betsy and Eric added three things. Include other states. Sort each topic by whether Ameren should lead, stay quiet, or only watch. And remember the bills can move fast. Rick raised local control, and Ben agreed to add it.

Treat the draft as a discussion draftBen's deck is not a company position. The notes are an automated summary, so names such as Crystal, Cheryl, and India may be transcription errors. Check a quote with the speaker before repeating it.

The information flow

  1. Collect. Everything people send, in one place.
  2. Label. Tag how we know it. Public, meeting, GR read, or working baseline.
  3. One page per topic. Fill the template and date it.
  4. Propose a lane. Active, passive, or watch, for Betsy and Eric to decide.
  5. Share and refresh. Send to Ben, then update weekly.

The draft slide on bring your own generation

The slide has three parts. A box on current rules lists CEJA (Public Act 102-0662), the IPA Adjustable Block Program, and MISO energy markets. A column of stakeholder positions covers environmental groups, data center operators, consumer advocates, and state agencies. A right column holds an issue overview, an Ameren Illinois position, and a possible compromise. The footer reads internal discussion draft, not an approved policy position.

What the slide saysWhat to flag
Legislators want data centers to supply or procure their own renewables.Betsy called this an oversimplification. Add the camp that wants own gas, including some legislators and labor.
The stakeholder list shows environmental groups, operators, consumer advocates, and state agencies.Labor, local governments, and the Governor's office are missing. Each shapes how this resolves.
Ameren Illinois position, support tiered self supply scaled to load size and interconnection speed.This is stated as a position. Label it a working baseline until the experts and Legal approve.
Possible compromise, a flexible interconnection exchange or a scaled requirement, crediting contracts, on site generation, or capacity.A good idea, but not a bill. Say who proposed it or mark it as our thinking.
CEJA already sets the emissions framework, so policy should focus on additionality and flexibility.This cuts two ways. Environmental groups call the POWER Act the floor and not the ceiling.

Four camps on bring your own power

Clean only

Environmental groups and the Governor's wording. New clean supply, no gas.

Own gas is fine

Some legislators and labor. Speed, jobs, and firm supply.

Pay, do not build

Operators. Credits, contracts, or a program fee.

Cost first

Consumer advocates and the Illinois Power Agency. Start with who pays.

Say it plainlyThe slide is a good start, but it reads as if the fight is clean versus fossil among legislators. Betsy made the point that some legislators and labor want data centers able to bring their own gas, so we need that camp on the page, and we should label our own line as a working baseline until it is approved.

Three lanes, used everywhere

Not every data center issue belongs to the utility. The site marks each one so you can tell, at a glance, where to speak up and where to listen.

Active Take a public stance

Issues that sit directly on the utility. Who pays for wires and substations, financial protection, who sets the rate, how fast a customer connects, and how the rules fit MISO.

Passive Hold a view, speak when it touches us

Issues that matter to the state but are not the utility's to lead. Water, air, local zoning, labor terms, biometric privacy. Others lead, and we speak if service, timing, or customer cost changes.

Watch Watch and prepare

Issues that could move up. Incentives, reporting, nondisclosure agreements, and clean energy matching. Track them, know the bill, and have a line ready.

Lanes are proposals for discussion, not company positions. The test for each one is on the Issues page.

The short version of the whole picture

What is happening

Illinois recruited data centers with tax incentives in 2019. AI scale projects arrived, and the questions changed. Who pays for the wires, how a project proves it is real, how reliability is protected, and what happens to water and neighbors. Fourteen bills were tracked this session and none settled it. On June 5 the Governor paused new incentive agreements and set out seven principles. The next opening is veto session.

Where the utility stands

Welcome credible investment. Make sure existing customers do not pay for projects that never arrive. Let the Illinois Commerce Commission set the tariff details on a full record. Respect customer choice. Remember that Ameren Illinois sits in MISO and ComEd sits in PJM, and a rule written for one can break the other.

Baseline language is a working position and has not been approved as company policy.

How many? It depends on who is counting.

There is no single authoritative public count. Each tracker defines a facility, building, campus, status, and capacity differently. The right habit is to name the dataset and the date, and to talk in megawatts by status when you can.

Status ladderInquiry, studied, contracted, energized, operating. About seven of every ten centers Aterio tracks in Illinois are not yet operating. Count the rung, not the announcement.

Where they are

Chicago core

Cook and DuPage counties hold most of the operating capacity, including Microsoft at Northlake and Hoffman Estates, QTS, Prime, Aligned, NTT, and CyrusOne in Aurora.

The collar counties

Kendall, Grundy, Will, and Lake counties hold the biggest planned numbers. A 1,800 MW Project Cardinal in Kendall County, Tract's 1,000 MW in Morris, Equinix at 700 MW and Cielo at 500 MW near Minooka, and T5's 1.2 GW campus in Grayslake.

Central and Metro East

Hut 8 in Logan County, CyrusOne in Sangamon County, Cloverleaf in Troy, and Project Chanel in Pontoon Beach. This is the Ameren Illinois footprint, and the projects are fewer but real.

Illinois sits on two grids

This is the single most important fact for anyone writing a statewide rule. The same data center request means different things north and south.

Say it plainlyPJM is asking who brings the capacity. MISO is asking whether the load will behave safely and whether anyone can see it. A rule written for one can break the other, and that is why statewide principles need regional implementation.

The incentive program and the pause

How it works

The 2019 Data Center Investment Program gives sales and use tax exemptions, and a construction wage credit in underserved areas. The statute asks for $250 million of investment over 60 months, at least 20 new qualifying jobs, and pay of at least 120 percent of the county average full time wage. Environmental certification and other conditions also apply.

20 ILCS 605/605-1025. A learning summary and not an eligibility checklist.

The pause

On June 5, 2026 the Governor directed the Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity to stop processing new agreements beginning July 1. Existing agreements are honored. A pause is not a repeal, and the statute still stands. DCEO's page says median where the statute says average, so use the legal text.

What it has cost

More than $983 million in reported incentives for 27 data centers from 2020 through 2024. That is a reported figure for those years and not the lifetime cost. Industry has warned the pause could discourage investment. Labor pushed back on it too.

The question now

Accountability. Compare verified investment, jobs, property tax, and community benefits against the exemption value and the infrastructure impact, then tie any future incentive to measurable performance. A tracker says the program has responsible bidder and project labor standards. Confirm that in the statute.

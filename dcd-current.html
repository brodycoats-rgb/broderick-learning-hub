The short version

Six things to know this week
  • The POWER Act had a Senate subject matter hearing on November 18 with no vote. House Majority Leader Gabel has said early January is more likely than veto session.
  • The pause on new state incentive agreements that began July 1 is still in place. The Governor won a third term on November 3 and said he wants data center legislation this fall.
  • In October FERC said that deciding which retail customers get curtailed belongs to the states. That puts more weight on Springfield and the ICC.
  • PJM and MISO filed their large load plans with FERC by November 16. Neither one requires a data center to bring its own capacity in order to connect. FERC has not ruled.
  • The U.S. Senate passed a permitting bill on November 19 that says data centers should carry their full grid costs. The House has not acted, and Democrats take control of both chambers in January.
  • Across the country, New York has a statewide moratorium by executive order, California and Florida passed cost laws, and voters opposed data centers in 40 of 41 ballot measures on November 3.

Where the disagreement is

  • What counts as bringing your own energy. Republican senators asked why nuclear and natural gas are excluded. Environmental groups oppose counting gas. Supporters signaled openness to nuclear.
  • Labor. Building trades unions have opposed versions of the bill. Climate Jobs Illinois argued at the hearing that the pause left ratepayers more exposed, not less.
  • Industry. The Data Center Coalition says it agrees with the goals but not the specific provisions, and wants a framework that separates good actors from bad ones.
  • Gas with carbon capture. Google has agreed to buy most of the output of the planned 400 MW Broadwing Energy Center in Decatur. Decatur residents and advocates protested at Google's Chicago office in November, and whether gas can count is a live question in the talks.

PJM and MISO

ComEd sits in PJM. Ameren Illinois sits in MISO Zone 4. The two grids moved in different directions this fall.

PJM, northern Illinois

  • July 14. The 2028/2029 capacity auction cleared 138,318 MW at the $325 per MW-day cap, 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement. PJM's no-cap simulation put the ComEd zone at $776.69.
  • Sept 29. FERC suspended PJM's backstop procurement for five months over cost allocation.
  • Oct 9. FERC accepted PJM's large load definition of 50 MW at a single site and a Large Load Registry, effective October 12. It rejected the interim curtailable service and the bring your own new capacity rules, finding they reached retail customers, and said curtailment choices belong to the states.
  • Nov 16. PJM's response proposes one 12 month study cluster for generation, transmission, and large loads, with curtailment through reliability orders. It does not require dedicated capacity to connect.
  • Dec 9. The 2029/2030 auction runs.

MISO, central and southern Illinois

  • April 28. The 2026/2027 auction showed enough capacity in every zone. Annualized prices were $116 to $126 per MW-day, down about 42 percent from the prior year. Summer cleared $424.30 in Zones 1 to 7.
  • Aug 28. MISO filed reliability rules for loads over 50 MW, with extra duties at 25 MW of IT equipment, covering data sharing, ramp limits, ride through, and monitoring. It asked for a December 4 effective date.
  • Nov 16. MISO's response proposes a 90 day large load study, a first application deadline of March 1, 2027, and three new transmission services. One is curtailable when prices pass $100 per MWh, starting January 1, 2029. It does not require dedicated capacity to connect.
Say it plainlyFERC sent the hardest question back to the states. Who gets curtailed, and who pays, is now a decision for Springfield and the ICC. PJM is short on capacity, while MISO cleared with room to spare this year, so one statewide rule will land differently north and south.

Washington

WhoWhatStatus
FERCDOE asked FERC in October 2025 to set national large load interconnection rules for loads of 20 MW or more. FERC did not issue a national rule. On June 18 it ordered all six regional grids to justify or reform their rules, with a preliminary large load line of 50 MW connected above 69 kV.Responses filed November 16, SPP November 20. Comments close between December 4 and January 15. Most grids asked for effective dates between February 1 and March 16, 2027.
CongressThe Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (S. 5653, passed using H.R. 2347) sets a national policy that data centers carry their full new grid and transmission costs, drops the earlier 20 MW threshold, gives FERC 90 days to issue a policy statement, creates a DOE program to give local governments independent analysis of proposed data centers, and puts RTO governance under FERC.Passed the Senate 62 to 33 on November 19. Now in the House with about 12 legislative days left as of November 20. If it does not pass, it starts over in the new Congress.
ElectionsDemocrats won majorities in the House and Senate on November 3. Sen. Husted of Ohio, lead Senate sponsor of the Ratepayer Protection Act, lost.New Congress starts in January 2027.
White HouseThe Ratepayer Protection Pledge, launched in March, asks data center companies to build, bring, or buy their own power and pay for grid upgrades. In July it grew to 23 Republican governors, 55 utilities, 106 cooperatives, and 28 developers.Voluntary and not binding.
White HouseA December 2025 executive order set up a task force to challenge state AI laws. Its stated framework would not preempt state rules on data center infrastructure, other than general permitting reform.Critics say the exemption is not guaranteed. Watch for any move against state data center laws.

Questions to watch

These are open questions for discussion, not company positions.

  • If an Illinois bill sets interruptible service or curtailment, does it fit MISO's new curtailable service as well as PJM's reliability orders?
  • Does new clean capacity in the POWER Act grow to include nuclear, or gas with carbon capture, as senators raised on November 18?
  • Does the ICC large load work stay with ComEd, or does it lead to a statewide approach that reaches Ameren Illinois?
  • If BAAJA becomes law, how would a FERC cost policy line up with any Illinois tariff?
  • Do the Madison County and other downstate referendum results change how members from the Ameren footprint vote?

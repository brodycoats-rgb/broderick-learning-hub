Data centers and hyperscalers

On site power is a bridge, one plan has three parts, and transmission agreements slow projects.

More on Data centers

What was said

Public views are mixed and confused. Speaker 1 said public backlash against data centers is real, and that calling them server farms raised positive views by 25 points, to 55 percent. No poll was supplied.

On site power is a bridge. Hyperscalers see generation behind the meter as a way to get power fast, not a long term answer, because they care most about reliability.

One framework has three parts. Speaker 4 described a structure like deals made with big technology firms. First, about 600 MW of plant upgrades, with 490 MW tied to Google and 30 MW to Walmart, paid for by the partners to buy turbines and build solar or clean supply. Second, 2,700 MW under a financial load following arrangement. Third, gigawatts of new nuclear or clean generation, or carbon capture, with Illinois competing against Texas, Utah and Arizona for new projects.

Developers hit roadblocks. Speakers 4 and 1 named transmission service agreements, security requirements, large up front transition costs, and labor union politics.

The meeting paused for lunch. It resumed after the break.

Why it matters

My reading

A data center buyer shops for speed, certainty and price. A state that cannot offer all three loses projects to states that can. The three part plan shows the difference between a contract on paper and steel in the ground, which is the distinction the old page's data center section builds on.

Say it in plain words

Data centers are unpopular in surveys, but calling them server farms reportedly raised approval by twenty five points, though no poll was shown. Companies that run them treat on site power as a bridge to get started fast. One plan described has three parts. About six hundred megawatts of plant upgrades, a twenty seven hundred megawatt financial supply arrangement that follows demand, and new nuclear or clean power over time. The roadblocks are transmission agreements, security deposits, big up front costs and labor politics, and Illinois is competing with Texas, Utah and Arizona.

Contract versus new plant

A financial load following deal moves risk and price around. It does not change how many megawatts the grid has at the hardest hour. A plant upgrade or a new unit does. Keep the two apart when you read any announcement.

Terms on this page

Hyperscaler From the meetings
A company that runs very large computing and data center sites, often for cloud services or artificial intelligence.
Behind the meter From the meetings
Power made on the customer's side of the utility meter, such as dedicated generators at a data center.
Load following From the meetings
A supply arrangement built around changing demand instead of one fixed volume.
Transmission service agreement From the meetings
A contract that sets commitments and financial duties for transmission service or for building needed lines.
Capacity Background
How much power can be counted on at the hardest moment. It is different from energy, which is how much is produced over time.

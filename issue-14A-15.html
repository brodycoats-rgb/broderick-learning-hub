Bears stadium legislation

Talks are stuck on property tax, schools, financing, and location. A location neutral bill is considered.

More on Labor, Bears, pensions

What was said

The outlook is unsettled. It is unclear whether the Bears will accept a public financing structure. Owners are weighing leaving the state. Speaker 1 said some would say to go to Indiana, because the team was never really in Illinois anyway.

Property tax is the flashpoint. Tax exemptions for a proposed $5 billion stadium draw intense pushback from the Chicago Teachers Union and school districts that would lose tax base.

A location neutral bill is considered. It would give legislators cover so they avoid voting explicitly to move the franchise. Speaker 2 has seen new language and said several options remain.

Past talks stalled. The Bears denied talks with the city while Arlington Heights suspected parallel talks. Frustration led to leadership changes among negotiators.

The old model was top down. The 2000s Soldier Field renovation worked because Governor George Ryan presented a single plan from the executive branch. Today the process is fragmented, and Governor Pritzker avoids leading talks to avoid the picture of a billionaire governor helping a billionaire family. Competing states, Indiana and Kansas, are recruiting.

A protective bill is reportedly in draft. The Senate President is said to be drafting a cover bill so members can defend a vote.

Why it matters

My reading

For a utility, the stadium fight matters because it uses the same limited attention and goodwill in Springfield that energy bills need. It is also a case study in how property tax and school funding arguments shape what can pass.

Say it in plain words

The Bears stadium is still unresolved. The big fights are property taxes, school funding, financing and location. A location neutral bill is being considered so legislators do not have to vote to move the team. The Soldier Field deal worked because the Governor led. Today no one leads. For us it is a reminder that this issue competes for the same political attention as energy.

Why property tax is the sticking point

School districts and local governments rely on property tax. An exemption for a very large project can cut their income for years, so they ask for replacement payments or other protections. Any workable bill has to answer that.

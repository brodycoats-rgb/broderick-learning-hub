What a data center is

A data center is a building, or a group of buildings, full of computers that store information, run software, and send results over the internet. Everything about the building exists to keep those computers running without a break. That means steady electricity, cooling, backup power, fast network lines, and security.

A campus can hold many buildings, which is why a count of buildings, campuses, and service requests can all describe the same project differently. When someone quotes a number, ask which one they are counting.

Hyperscale

Very large campuses built by or for the biggest cloud and AI companies. Hundreds of megawatts, sometimes a gigawatt. There is no agreed megawatt line, and bills define it differently.

Colocation

A landlord builds the shell, power, and cooling, and rents space to many customers. Many of Illinois' operating sites near Chicago work this way.

Enterprise and edge

A company's own computer rooms, or small sites close to users. These are small in megawatts and large in number, which is why site counts and megawatts tell different stories.

Crypto mining is not the same thingCryptocurrency mining sites use a lot of power but are a different business with different noise and job patterns. SB3030 is about them. Do not apply every data center provision to them, or the reverse.

What it does

When you stream a show, check a bank balance, or ask an AI tool a question, a data center does the work. Two kinds of work drive the recent surge in requests.

  • Training builds an AI model. It runs thousands of chips at once for weeks, so it needs very large, steady power in one place.
  • Serving answers questions from the finished model. It is spread across many sites and grows with how much people use the tools.

Both need a customer that draws power around the clock. That steady draw is what makes a data center different from a factory that shuts down at night.

A power primer in five lines

MW and MWh

A megawatt is power at one moment. A megawatt hour is energy over time. A steady 50 MW load for 24 hours uses 1,200 MWh. A 50 MW announcement alone does not tell you the annual total.

IT load and facility load

IT load is the computers. Facility load adds cooling and power conversion. The ratio is called PUE. A lower PUE means less overhead, not necessarily less total power or water.

Requested and operating

A service request is an input to planning. It is not proof of a financed, built, energized project. Several possible sites can describe one prospect.

Energy and capacity

Energy is what is produced or used over time. Capacity is the ability to meet demand under planning rules. Buying clean energy for a year does not prove power is available in every hour.

The money

There is no single trustworthy number for what has been invested in data centers. Different sources count buildings, chips, and power equipment differently, and most announced figures are plans across many years.

$410B to $725B

Capital spending by the four largest US technology companies, roughly $410 billion last year and guided toward about $725 billion in 2026. Most is tied to AI. It covers chips and servers as well as buildings, and it is spent around the world.

Reported by Tom's Hardware and Silicon Canals from company guidance.

$10B

The reported cost of the Compass campus in Hoffman Estates, with megawatts not stated. The biggest dollar figure on the Illinois list.

Playbook, Section 3.

About $1B

Expected investment in Project Chanel (DAMAC Digital) at Pontoon Beach, with 500 MW of contracted first phase load. The largest new business project in the Ameren Illinois footprint.

Knowledge center, Appendix G.

Rules for any investment number
  • Say whether it is announced, committed, or spent.
  • Say whether it includes chips and servers or only the building and power.
  • Say over how many years.
  • Compare the jobs after construction with the construction jobs. Permanent staffing is usually small next to the investment.

Illinois has reported more than $983 million in incentives for 27 data centers from 2020 through 2024. That is a reported figure for those years and not the lifetime cost of the program. More on the Illinois page.

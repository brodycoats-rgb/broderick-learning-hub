Spring session recap and lobbying

Engage lawmakers early and give each bill a named lead across member companies.

More on Legislative strategy

What was said

The association shifted from waiting to engaging early. Speaker 1 described the new approach as arriving before others do and asking a legislator, in effect, whether they really want a bill that has problems. Often the answer is a request for help fixing it.

About 70 percent of the time the association can help. In about 30 percent of cases the bill is too problematic. This is the speaker's experience, not a measured rate.

Silos inside companies get in the way. Speaker 1 said regulatory staff, lawyers and engineers can each believe they would make a better lobbyist, and each may talk to legislators. When that happens, lawmakers can wrongly assume a company supports a bill based on a conversation nobody authorized.

The bill submission process needs a paper trail. The new workflow should send an automatic email confirmation when a bill is submitted, so there is a record and a way to follow amendments.

Every bill should have a named lead. Speaker 1 and Speaker 4 talked about tracking who leads what, with Brad leading the Next Generation Highways bill as the example. Speaker 1 suggested a shared assignment sheet across companies, Ameren among them, naming the lead for topics such as generation or low income issues.

Why it matters

My reading

One voice per bill is the discipline behind everything else in these meetings. Without it, legislators hear several versions of the same company and trust none of them. The assignment sheet is a small tool that makes that discipline visible.

Say it in plain words

The association changed its approach. Instead of waiting to react, it now goes to legislators early and offers to fix problems in a bill. About seventy percent of the time that works, based on the speaker's experience. The trouble is inside the companies. When regulatory, legal and engineering staff each talk to lawmakers, legislators may think the company supports something it never approved. The fix is a clear lead for every bill, an email confirmation when a bill is submitted, and a shared sheet that shows who leads each topic across companies.

Why one voice wins

A legislator who hears two answers from one company does not average them. They tend to assume the least helpful one. A single named lead, who has the authority to say yes or no, removes that risk and builds trust over time.

The new Congress and affordability

Affordability is the main theme of the new Congress, and the energy piece of it has an Illinois lead who represents Ameren territory.

The overall frame

Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries created five working groups on health care, housing, groceries and goods, gas and utilities, and caregiving, each led by a progressive and a moderate. Democrats say they will start the agenda in the first 100 hours after the new Congress is seated on Jan 3. The first bills are mostly health care. In those first 100 hours, House Democrats plan to vote on bills reinstating the enhanced health insurance subsidies that expired, and reversing the Medicaid cuts Trump signed into law (CNBC).

For the full first 100 days, Jeffries has listed reversing the Medicaid cuts, restoring SNAP funding, extending the Affordable Care Act tax credits, ending the Trump tariffs and stopping the war in Iran. He has also promised an early bill banning members of Congress from trading stock. Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar sums it up as lowering costs, protecting health care and rooting out corruption.

The energy piece, and the Ameren connection

  • Budzinski co-leads the group. Jeffries has vowed to quickly push an affordability agenda with energy as a key pillar. He tasked Reps. Nikki Budzinski of Illinois and Mike Levin of California with leading a Gas and Utilities Affordability Working Group that has crafted ideas, though they remain under wraps (Axios, Nov 4). Budzinski represents IL-13, which is Ameren territory.
  • No detailed plan yet. A detailed Democratic energy agenda hasn't emerged, but there are early clues. Senate Leader Chuck Schumer tied Republicans to electricity costs on election night and has named grid development, consumer protection, clean energy, more efficiency support and clearer utility bills as goals (Axios).
  • Casten's Energy Bills Relief Act. Another Illinois member, Rep. Sean Casten, introduced this bill in March with Levin. It is backed by more than half of all House Democrats and would reverse the Republican rollback of solar and wind tax credits and encourage permits for wind and solar.
  • Other idea sources. The New Democrat Coalition's affordability agenda includes a plank to change how electric grid costs are allocated, and Illinois Rep. Brad Schneider helped roll it out. A separate group of nine House Democrats put out a "policy blueprint for 2027" with over 800 recommendations on issues including data centers and energy security. The 100-plus member Sustainable Energy and Environment Coalition backed hundreds more, and some, like advanced nuclear, could draw Republican votes.
  • Committee chair. Frank Pallone, the New Jersey Democrat expected to chair the House Energy and Commerce Committee, says the party should focus on health care, affordability, consumer and environmental protections, clean energy and regulating Big Tech (PBS).

The limits

  • Enacting major changes would require Republican support, because Democrats will lack the numbers to override presidential vetoes (PBS).
  • Even then, lower energy bills are not guaranteed, because prices also depend on fuel costs, demand and the expense of maintaining the electric grid (PBS).
  • The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimates the first-100-days agenda could increase primary deficits by about 4.1 trillion dollars through fiscal year 2037.
  • Democrats are split between an "abundance" wing that wants to build faster and progressives who want a harder line on fossil fuels, which could pull the energy agenda in different directions (Axios).

For Ameren

The working group's ideas on grid cost allocation and data centers could land on utility bills directly, and they would overlap with the POWER Act debate in Springfield and with MISO capacity costs. Budzinski is the natural first call, since she represents Ameren customers and helped write the energy ideas. Jeffries plans to formally unveil the full legislative program in early December, ahead of the Jan 3 swearing-in, so the window to weigh in is the next few weeks.

Suggested follow-upAsk the federal affairs team to request a staff briefing with Budzinski's office before the agenda is unveiled, and confirm Ameren's position on the permitting bill below before the House votes.

Key federal dates

Also before January, the permitting bill

The outgoing Congress may pass a major energy bill before Democrats take over. The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA) 62 to 33 on Nov 19. It was written by Sens. Capito, Lee, Whitehouse and Heinrich.

  • It overhauls federal permitting for energy and infrastructure, with concurrent environmental review, more public disclosure of permits and new rules for transmission landowner notices (Holland and Knight).
  • It includes data center ratepayer protections. Reporting says data centers would cover the cost of their electricity and the transmission built to serve them. Confirm the text with the federal team.
  • Speaker Johnson plans a two-thirds suspension vote after Thanksgiving, then a regular rule if that fails. Jeffries wants more House input, and any House change sends it back to the Senate.
  • Senate negotiators want it attached to the Dec 11 funding bill. Unions and the clean energy industry support it, and many environmental groups are undecided or opposed.

Who to watch

  • Rep. Nikki Budzinski (IL-13). Co-leads the gas and utilities working group. Represents Ameren customers.
  • Rep. Sean Casten (IL-6). Lead author of the Energy Bills Relief Act.
  • Rep. Brad Schneider (IL-10). Helped roll out the New Democrat affordability agenda.
  • Rep. Frank Pallone. Expected Energy and Commerce chair. Watch his first hearings in January for utility rates and data centers.
  • Sen. Chuck Schumer. Expected Senate Majority Leader, pledging consumer protection from rising energy prices.
  • Sen.-elect Juliana Stratton. Takes office in January as Illinois's newest senator.

2What happens next

The outgoing General Assembly has one more veto session week, then a January lame duck session before the new members are sworn in on Jan 13. Here is the calendar, what happened in week one, and the Ameren to-do list.

Key dates

Veto session, week one (Nov 17 to 19)

  • Lawmakers wrapped up the first week without major action (WTTW, Nov 19).
  • The Chicago Bears stadium bill drew most of the attention. It passed the Senate in the spring and still needs the House (Sun-Times, Nov 18).
  • A Senate committee held a subject matter hearing on the POWER Act data center bill on Nov 18, the same day advocates held a lobby day at the Capitol.
  • Sen. Bill Cunningham said data center legislation will likely wait until January (Sun-Times, Nov 18).

Veto session, week two (Dec 1 to 3)

  • Items to watch are data center regulation, more money for public schools and the Bears stadium (WTTW).
  • Anything meant for veto session must pass both chambers by Dec 3 (Axios).
  • House Democrats hear from Speaker candidates during this week, with the vote in January (Sun-Times).
  • If data center rules slip, the next windows are the January lame duck session and the new General Assembly's spring session.

What it means for Ameren Illinois

  • Democrats grow their three-fifths supermajorities to 84 in the House (71 needed) and 45 in the Senate (36 needed). Leaders need even fewer Republican votes for veto overrides and for bills passed after May 31.
  • Data center rules are most likely to land in January or the spring session, not this month. Keep messages ready for both.
  • A new Speaker means new committee chairs and possibly a new House point person on energy.
  • Republican leaders stayed in place, so Republican energy contacts do not change.

Next steps

    4Energy and other major issues

    Electricity bills and data centers are where energy policy and politics meet. Start with the data center bill, then tap any tile below for what it means, why it is hot, who says what and what to watch.

    The POWER Act, where it stands on Nov 25

    What it would do
    • Require large data centers to bring their own clean energy.
    • Bar shifting their costs onto other customers.
    • Require disclosure of water and electricity use, and efficient cooling.
    • End nondisclosure agreements between data centers and local governments.

    Senate Bill 4016 and House Bill 5513 (Yahoo News via Capitol News Illinois, Axios).

    Where it stands
    • It stalled in the spring session.
    • Gov. Pritzker paused new data center tax incentives starting July 1.
    • A Senate committee held a subject matter hearing Nov 18.
    • Sen. Cunningham expects action to wait until January.
    Who says what
    • Pritzker says new data centers should bring their own clean energy, help keep bills down and use closed-loop water.
    • Building trades unions oppose the bill. Industry groups support its goals but want changes (Axios, Nov 16).
    • Advocates call it making Big Tech pay its fair share.

    Ameren Illinois supply price

    Price to Compare for the first 800 kWh a month, in cents per kWh

    5Chicago mayor, Feb 23, 2027

    Sixteen candidates filed. The ballot is set Dec 17 after petition challenges, the first round is Feb 23, and a runoff follows on Apr 6 if no one tops 50 percent.

    Who makes the ballot

    • 16 candidates filed petitions by the Oct 26 deadline. Each needs 12,500 valid signatures.
    • 12 of them face petition challenges, including Johnson, Giannoulias, Mendoza, Quigley and Pappas (NBC Chicago).
    • Willie Wilson's campaign challenged Johnson's petitions and estimates only about 5,000 of his signatures are valid. Johnson's campaign says he will have more than enough (Fox 32, NBC Chicago).
    • Candidate Bill Quinlan objected to Giannoulias, Quigley and Pappas, arguing in part that Quigley and Pappas should not be on the ballot because they also sought reelection in November.
    • The review of Johnson's petitions continues into December (Block Club Chicago, Nov 24). The board certifies the ballot by Dec 17.

    Johnson is the first sitting mayor to face a petition challenge since 2007 (Block Club Chicago).

    The 2027 city budget fight

    • Johnson proposed a 17.6 billion dollar budget on Oct 21 that revives his corporate head tax (WTTW, Sun-Times).
    • On Nov 2 the City Council voted 44 to 6 to put a 75 million dollar parking meter payment toward pensions, opening a new hole in his plan.
    • On Nov 10, 28 alderpeople told Johnson the plan does not have the votes and ruled out a property tax increase.
    • The Council must pass a budget by the end of December, two months before the election.

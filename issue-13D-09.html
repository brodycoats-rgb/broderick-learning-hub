Large load tariffs and grid plans

The ICC asked Ameren and ComEd for large load tariffs. Final grid plan orders are expected in December 2026.

More on Plans and regulation

What was said

The ICC asked for large load tariffs. Speaker 1 said the commission asked Ameren and ComEd to file new tariffs for very large customers.

The aim is interruptibility. The tariffs would separate big industrial and data center demand and allow the state to shut off data centers during high grid strain.

The grid plans are in the final stretch. Ameren ran into an administrative law judge issue with preliminary findings, and ComEd had no immediate update. Final rulings on the multi year plans are expected by December 2026.

Utilities have to wait to speak. They are limited in outside communication until final orders are issued, and legislative outreach will follow.

The decision was to defer broad outreach. The group will wait for the December orders.

Why it matters

My reading

Two separate decisions are being made, how much to spend on the grid and who pays for the largest new customers. The legislature will want to react to both, but utilities cannot talk freely until the orders land. That is why December is a hinge date.

Say it in plain words

The Illinois Commerce Commission asked Ameren and ComEd to file tariffs just for very large customers like data centers, so the state can set rules for interrupting them under grid stress. Final rulings on the multi year grid plans are expected in December, and until then the utilities are limited in what they can say. We will hold broad legislative outreach until those orders are out.

Why a separate tariff for large users

A very large customer can change what a utility must build. A dedicated tariff lets the regulator set contract length, minimum payments, and interruption rules for that customer class, so other customers are not left holding the cost.

ICC Background
The Illinois Commerce Commission, which oversees retail utility rates and plans.

A hearing officer who reviews a case at a regulator and issues findings before the commission decides.

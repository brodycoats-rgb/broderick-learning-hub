Wetlands and Senate leadership

Senator Elman plans to bring back a state wetlands bill, and cooling ponds could be swept in. State mapping is expected this month.

More on Highways, wetlands, heat

What was said

Senator Elman plans to bring back a state wetlands bill. The push follows US Supreme Court rulings that narrowed federal reach over wetlands, which leaves states to decide whether to fill the gap.

Broad definitions could sweep in utility assets. Speaker 1 warned that cooling ponds could fall under some definitions. Plants that sit directly on rivers may be less affected.

The Farm Bureau led the opposition last time and feels confident. The group agreed that utilities need to take part together and not rely on others to carry the point.

State mapping is close. IDNR was expected to share survey and mapping results this month, based on talks with the interim director.

Committee leadership is unsettled. The group talked about Senator Elman, a former chair named Hastings, and a senator who may become a contract lobbyist.

The plan is a working group. One representative per utility would draft language and define operating boundaries before the veto session and the spring session.

Why it matters

My reading

The words in a definition decide which ponds, ditches and basins count. If cooling ponds are in, a plant could face permits and limits it never expected. The IDNR map is the first hard evidence of how far the state may go, so it is worth reading the day it appears.

Say it in plain words

Senator Elman plans to bring back a state wetlands bill because recent Supreme Court rulings cut back federal protection. For utilities the risk sits in the definitions. If cooling ponds count as wetlands, plants could face new permits and limits. The Farm Bureau led the opposition before, but utilities need to speak together. The state natural resources department is expected to release its wetlands mapping this month, and we plan to form a small working group, one person per utility, to draft language and set clear operating boundaries before the veto session and the spring session.

Why a federal ruling leads to a state bill

When federal protection shrinks, advocates often ask the state to write its own rule. That moves the fight to the General Assembly, where definitions, exemptions and mapping authority are all negotiable. Utilities want those words settled in a way that fits how plants actually operate.

What a working group should settle first

Which assets are in question, which are already permitted under other programs, and what language would exempt engineered cooling features. Keep a short list of assets so the group argues from facts.

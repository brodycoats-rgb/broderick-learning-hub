The honest starting point

Most people who try to day trade lose money. A large study of Brazilian futures day traders found that nearly all who kept at it for more than 300 days lost money, and only about 1 percent earned more than minimum wage. A long study of Taiwan day traders found that fewer than 1 percent were reliably profitable after costs.

So the goal of this program is not to make you a gambler with better vocabulary. The goal is to make you someone who understands how stocks, rates and futures connect, who manages risk like a professional, and who can explain all of it clearly. That last skill is what gets people on television.

Sources are the Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti study "Day Trading for a Living?" and the Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean work on Taiwan day traders.

Your real edge for Bloomberg

Bloomberg bookers want guests with a lane. Strategists, economists, portfolio managers, CEOs and policy experts get booked because they know something specific that moves markets.

You already have a lane most market people do not. You know how data center load, MISO capacity prices, utility capex, rate cases and state energy law actually work. Hyperscalers like Meta are spending enormous sums on AI data centers, and power is now a market story. The person who can connect a Meta capex number to a capacity auction result to a utility stock is rare.

Your positioning line. "I translate energy policy into what it means for markets."

Three rules that protect you

Your job creates insider risk. In government relations you often know about legislation, dockets and policy before the public. Trading Ameren, peer utilities, power producers or energy names on that knowledge can be illegal. Read Ameren's securities trading policy, follow any preclearance and blackout rules, and when in doubt, do not trade those names.
Clear media with your employer. Companies usually require approval from corporate communications before an employee speaks to press, even in a personal capacity. Ask before your first interview.
Paper first. Log 60 paper trades with a written plan before risking real money. If your expectancy is not positive after 60, keep learning.

An index is a basket of stocks with one number that tells you how the whole basket did. When an anchor says "the market is up," they mean one of these baskets. Know what is inside each one and you will know why it moved.

Who is at the top

S&P 500

Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft have been the three largest companies, together about a fifth of the whole index in October 2026. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Broadcom and Tesla round out most of the top 10.

The top 10 companies are close to 40 percent of the index, the most concentrated it has ever been. In the late 1990s tech bubble the peak was about 27 percent.

Meta was about 2.6 percent of the index in October 2026. Weights change every day. The live map is always current.

Nasdaq-100

Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Broadcom dominate. The top 10 are a little under half the index.

Nasdaq caps the biggest weights so the index does not become just a handful of stocks. The list is rebuilt every December.

Your angle. A few power companies listed on Nasdaq, such as Constellation Energy, sit inside this "tech" index. Power is part of the AI story.

Dow

Because it is price weighted, the highest-priced shares, such as Goldman Sachs, move it most.

The committee changes members rarely. Nvidia replaced Intel in November 2024. Alphabet replaced Verizon on June 29, 2026. Apple, Microsoft and Amazon are members. Meta is not.

The concentration story on air. "The S&P is really a bet on a handful of AI companies. The top 10 stocks are close to 40 percent of the index. When Nvidia reports, the whole market reports."

Beyond the big five

Other U.S. gauges

  • S&P 500 Equal Weight, ETF RSP. Every company counts the same. When it lags the regular S&P, a few giants are carrying the market.
  • S&P MidCap 400, ETF MDY. Mid-sized companies.
  • VIX. The "fear gauge," built from S&P 500 option prices. It rises when stocks fall hard.

Global indexes

  • Nikkei 225, Japan
  • Hang Seng, Hong Kong
  • DAX, Germany
  • FTSE 100, United Kingdom
  • Stoxx Europe 600, broad Europe

Asia trades during your evening. Europe opens around 2 a.m. CT. Check both before the U.S. open.

Bond and commodity indexes

  • Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate, ETFs AGG and BND. The benchmark for investment-grade U.S. bonds.
  • Bloomberg Commodity Index. A basket of energy, metals and crops.
  • Dollar index, DXY. The dollar against six major currencies, mostly the euro.

How companies get in and out

  • The S&P 500 is run by a committee at S&P Dow Jones Indices. A company needs to be large, U.S.-based, liquid and profitable. Its reported earnings must be positive over the last four quarters combined and in the latest quarter.
  • The S&P rebalances quarterly, on the third Friday of March, June, September and December, and adds companies anytime a seat opens.
  • The Nasdaq-100 is rebuilt once a year in December by market value.

The index effect. Trillions of dollars in index funds must buy a stock when it joins and sell when it leaves. Additions often jump when they are announced. The effect has gotten smaller as traders front-run it.

Futures and the open. S&P and Nasdaq futures trade overnight, so "futures are up" at 7 a.m. CT is your early read on how the indexes will open.

12-week plan

About five hours a week. Check items off as you go. Each week repeats the daily routine, so the habits stack.

Credentials and next steps after week 12

  • FINRA SIE exam. The Securities Industry Essentials exam is open to anyone 18 or older with no firm sponsor. It proves you know the basics of products, markets and rules.
  • Bloomberg Market Concepts. Bloomberg's self-paced course on economics, currencies, fixed income and equities, taken on a Bloomberg Terminal. Many university and public business libraries have terminals. Ask WashU Olin about alumni access.
  • Publish weekly. A short "Energy and Markets" note on LinkedIn or your hub. Bookers look for people who already explain things well in public.
  • Build the ladder. Trade press like Utility Dive, then local business radio and TV in St. Louis, Springfield and Peoria, then podcasts, then Bloomberg Radio and TV. Each clip becomes proof for the next booking.

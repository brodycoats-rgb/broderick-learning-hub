Extreme temperature rules

The bill adds breaks, water, and facilities. Several industries object, and the advice was to work with the wider business community.

More on Highways, wetlands, heat

What was said

The bill adds required breaks, water and facilities for people working in extreme temperatures. Senator Moynihan was named in the notes.

Several industries push back. Airlines, motion pictures and utility field operations were all mentioned.

Some local governments with large immigrant workforces are supportive. Speaker 1 said the idea has traction in those settings.

Do not ask for a utility only exemption. Speaker 1 argued that asking for acceptance of a utility carve out will not go far, and that the better path is to fall back on the wider business community and hold the same line they do.

Legislators misread what drives utility costs. Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 talked about how lawmakers misjudge utility cost drivers and compliance expenses.

Why it matters

My reading

Crews work outdoors through storms and heat waves, and restoration often cannot wait for a scheduled break. A broad business coalition has more weight than a single industry asking for special treatment, and it avoids looking self interested.

Say it in plain words

An extreme temperature bill would require more breaks, water and facilities for outdoor workers. Airlines, film production and utility field crews are all uneasy. The advice from the room was not to ask for a utility only exemption, because that rarely works. Instead, stand with the wider business community and push for the same changes they want. We should also help lawmakers see what actually drives utility costs and what compliance would add.

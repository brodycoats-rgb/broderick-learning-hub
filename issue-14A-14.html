Renewable siting and the Supreme Court

A ruling expected before year end could limit state preemption, and some tax exemptions look shaky.

More on Solar and siting

What was said

The Illinois Supreme Court is deciding a siting case. It concerns whether the state may override local county and municipal zoning of renewable energy. A ruling is expected before the end of 2026.

The risk is a patchwork. If the Court rules the General Assembly cannot restrict local zoning, statewide transmission and renewable siting would face serious disruption.

Special legislation is a second worry. Legal counsel called state statutes “pretty shaky” on generation tax exemptions, which could violate the constitutional ban on special legislation.

A Danforth case was cited. A case near Danforth was named as a key precedent.

Why it matters

My reading

Developers finance projects on the assumption of one set of statewide rules. If counties can set their own, costs and timing differ place to place. The tax exemption question is a separate legal issue that could hit project economics directly.

Say it in plain words

The Illinois Supreme Court is considering whether the state can override local zoning for renewable projects. A ruling is expected by the end of the year. If the court says no, developers would face different rules county by county. Counsel also thinks some generation tax exemptions look shaky under the special legislation ban. We should find the exact case, watch for the ruling, and prepare for scenarios.

Background General context I added. It was not said in the meeting.

Two separate legal questions

One is zoning preemption, whether the state can limit what counties may do. The other is special legislation, whether a law gives particular groups different treatment that the constitution does not allow. A ruling on one does not decide the other.

Terms on this page

Preemption From the meetings
When state law overrides certain local rules.
Special legislation From the meetings
A constitutional concern when lawmakers give particular groups different legal treatment in ways the constitution may not allow.

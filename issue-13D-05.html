Voluntary versus involuntary curtailment

The preference is voluntary programs, with payment if industrial load is cut by force.

More on Plans and regulation

What was said

Industrial load that can be interrupted is growing. The speaker said more industrial customers are able to be interrupted.

Gas phase out raises curtailment risk. The speaker thinks regulators will take as much as they can from interruptible load as they try to shut down gas.

The political risk is clear. If there is a grid problem, shedding industrial market load could be a political problem, but the speaker would rather shed industrial load than other customers.

Voluntary beats involuntary. The stated preference is for voluntary programs, and for paying customers who are cut off against their will.

The commission wants everyone included. The speaker said they met with the commission, raised the idea of involuntary curtailment, and heard that the commission wants everybody covered.

A selling point was offered. “Bring your company to our state where we will keep the lights on.”

Why it matters

My reading

Large customers choose where to locate partly on the chance of being cut off. A program they join and are paid for is a feature. A forced cut with no payment is a reason to go elsewhere. The compensation question is the center of this debate.

Say it in plain words

Interruptible industrial load is growing, and a gas phase out would make cuts more likely. The speaker prefers voluntary programs, with payment if a customer is cut involuntarily, and warns of political fallout if the grid sheds industrial load without a plan. The commission seems to want to cover everybody. Our job is to design options with clear triggers and fair payment, and to prepare materials for the commission.

Voluntary and involuntary programs

A voluntary program pays a customer in advance to agree to reduce use when called. An involuntary cut happens without that agreement, usually as a last resort. The first is a product. The second is an emergency measure that needs rules about who goes first and what they are owed.

Terms on this page

Interruptibility From the meetings
The ability to reduce a customer's power during a shortage, usually in return for a lower price or a payment.
Interruptible tariff From the meetings
A rate that lets a utility reduce a customer's supply under set conditions, usually in return for a lower price.

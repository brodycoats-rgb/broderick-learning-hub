Statewide and federal election outlook

Democrats are expected to hold statewide offices and Stratton is favored for the U.S. Senate seat. A new Comptroller is expected.

More on Elections

What was said

Democrats are expected to keep every statewide office. Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 reviewed projections that favor the party across the statewide executive seats.

The slate has shifted. Speaker 2 pointed to Christian Mitchell as the Lieutenant Governor candidate while the current Lieutenant Governor, Juliana Stratton, runs for federal office.

Comptroller and Treasurer look like the status quo. A new Comptroller is expected.

A Chicago mayoral run was discussed. Speaker 2 said the Secretary of State is running for Mayor of Chicago, and that if he won, the Governor would appoint a replacement in 2027. The source notes say this should be treated as speculation, since he remains a candidate for reelection.

The U.S. Senate race favors the Democrat. Speaker 2 assessed that Juliana Stratton is favored over Don Tracy in the race to succeed Senator Dick Durbin.

Campaign filings are the signal to watch. Brad was named to track D-2 filings for Q3 and pre election spending across statewide and mayoral operations.

Why it matters

My reading

Continuity in the executive branch means CEJA implementation, grid modernization and environmental policy are likely to continue in their current direction. The Governor also sets the tone for deals like the Bears stadium and Tier 2, which the same meeting discussed.

Say it in plain words

Participants expect Democrats to keep every statewide office, and they see Juliana Stratton as the favorite for the U.S. Senate seat Dick Durbin is leaving. A new Comptroller is expected. There was talk of the Secretary of State running for Chicago mayor, but that is speculation and he is still on the ballot for Secretary of State. Brad will watch campaign finance reports for signals about which races each side thinks are close.

What a D-2 can show

An Illinois D-2 report lists contributions and spending. Late spending often shows where a party believes a race is close, even when public statements sound confident.

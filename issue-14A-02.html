State Senate races

Districts 23, 26, 48, and 56 drew the most attention, with ad spending as the signal.

More on Elections

What was said

Several senators are leaving. Speaker 2 reviewed departures, with reasons that include a lost congressional primary and retirement. The names as transcribed are Feinstein, Walker, Fowler, Cunningham and Holmes, and the spellings are unverified.

District 48, Turner against Lesko. Senator Turner faces a Republican challenger in a Springfield to Decatur district that was redrawn evenly in 2022. Her campaign is scaling back broadcast TV buys, which Speaker 2 read as confidence.

District 26, Darby against Saeed. The notes say incumbent Darby faces Rep. Nabeela Saeed in a seat that leans slightly Republican after redistricting. Official records list Darby Hills and Nabeela Syed. Democrats have kept up TV advertising, and Speaker 1 said Darby missed a League of Women Voters forum for family reasons.

District 23, Lewis against McAdams. Republican Senator Seth Lewis faces a DSA backed challenger. Speaker 2 reported Lewis exceeded contribution caps through a self loan. The state party increased mailings, but Senate Democrats gave only $2,500 in kind support, reflecting Lewis's moderate profile. The seat is projected to stay Republican.

District 33 and other open seats. The seat replacing Senator Inouye was among the open contests, and the next D-2 reports should show staff and mail spending.

District 56, Harriss against Gullett Murphy. Senator Erica Harriss faces a former IDOT engineer. The electorate is union dense and swing prone, and voted for Trump in 2018, yet the district tilts slightly Republican.

Caucus friction. Speakers 1 and 3 discussed leadership aspirations in the Senate Democratic caucus, naming Stevenson and Resn as transcribed.

Why it matters

My reading

A small number of Senate seats can reshape committee membership and who carries energy bills. Ad spending is the clearest public signal of where each side believes a race is close, and it costs nothing to watch.

Say it in plain words

The Senate races worth watching are Districts 23, 26, 48 and 56. District 48 looks safe for the incumbent, since her campaign is cutting TV. District 26 leans slightly Republican but Democrats keep advertising. District 23 is projected to stay Republican because the incumbent is moderate and Democrats are spending little. District 56 leans Republican even though it is union heavy. The takeaway is that spending, not talk, is the signal.

Executive engagement

Take the severity of the situation to CEOs and CFOs.

More on People and organization

What was said

Speaker 2 asked for any more updates. The call for additional items on the veto session is partly garbled in the notes. Speaker 4 then proposed a short break.

The meeting closed with an urgent request. Speaker 2 strongly urged the team to convey how serious the situation is to senior leadership, naming the CEO and the CFO.

Why it matters

My reading

Retention costs, closure risk, capacity charges and labor costs are company wide decisions. They cannot be solved by legislative or regulatory teams alone. A short, clear executive brief puts the money and dates in front of the people who can decide.

Say it in plain words

The last request of the afternoon was direct. Make sure your chief executive and chief financial officer understand how serious this is. The grid risk, the plant retirements and the labor developments all carry costs that only senior leaders can decide on. A one page brief with the capacity at risk, the key dates, the dollars exposed, and a clear ask is the best way to do that.

A simple outline for a one page brief

Start with what is at risk, in megawatts and in dollars. Add the dates that force a decision, such as the draft mitigation plan and the veto session. Say what you recommend. End with who needs to act and by when. This outline is my suggestion and was not in the meeting.

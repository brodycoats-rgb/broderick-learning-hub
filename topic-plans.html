Plants, workforce, and fuel

Oct 13

Plants still need many workers and the ability to develop fuel. The speaker said nothing has changed about the plants, with staffing gaps and fuel supply still a concern. Competitive markets reward plants that can switch on and off, such as gas plants or wind turbines. The stated goal was to start with policies that bring investors to the state, to be discussed at a regular meeting. The notes do not say which plants have the biggest gaps. Suggested follow ups were a hiring plan and a fuel roadmap, but those are not assignments.

Plans with dates attached

Oct 13

The ICC asked Ameren and ComEd to file large load tariffs. The purpose is to set apart very large customers such as data centers and to set up large load interruptibility, which would let the state reduce those customers' supply when the grid is under strain. Final rulings on the multi-year grid plans are expected in December 2026.

Oct 13

Utilities are limited in what they can say until the orders come. Ameren ran into a judge related issue with preliminary findings, and ComEd had no update. The group chose to hold broad legislative outreach until the final orders are issued, then proceed.

Oct 13

The IRP and CEJA mitigation plan have a data problem. Models showed out of state power imports tripling or quadrupling over five to ten years. The state counted about 800 MW of Constellation upgrades, but PJM saw that most of those megawatts are already spoken for. The IRP is expected to be public the day before the veto session, with no preview for the working group, and the legislature is not expected to act right away. The group plans to prepare a response before the document appears.

Capacity, procurement, and who has the authority

Oct 13

PJM is caught up on its three year forward procurement. Auction results are expected near the end of the session. A backstop procurement is expected within five to six months, around March and April 2027, with heavy cost shares for large Illinois energy users. MISO is projected to face large capacity challenges and rule changes in its next procurement. The exact charge to large users is not known.

Oct 13

A one time six gigawatt option and ComEd cost allocation came up, but the transcript is garbled. The notes point to a one time option, cost rules for customers in the ComEd zone, a 13 percent figure, and heavy reliance on an interruptible proposal. The meaning of the veto set and the PM process is also unclear. Treat everything in this item as unconfirmed.

Oct 13

Who can order a purchase or a tariff was debated. The speaker doubted that the IRP gives anyone authority to require customers or utilities to buy power, and said the commission clearly can stop a request. The speaker also said they have opposed interruptibility tariffs and tariffs for basic facilities. That is one person's stated position, not a company decision.

Oct 13

Industrial load curtailment, voluntary or involuntary. Interruptible industrial load is growing. The stated preference was voluntary programs, with payment if a customer is cut involuntarily. The speaker said a recent meeting with the commission raised the idea of involuntary curtailment, and that the commission seemed to want to include everyone. The line "bring your company to our state where we will keep the lights on" was offered as a selling point.

Gas, coal, and the fleet

Oct 13

A full natural gas utility phase out by 2050 was pushed back. The ICC Chairman will publish an advisory report with recommendations instead of imposing a binding phase out. The working group wants any legislation to use aspirational targets, not hard bans. The report date is unknown.

Oct 13

Several plant ownership and retirement points came up. Combined cycle gas units face less pressure before 2040, and operators are organizing for IRP input. A working group of combined cycle operators was planned. Names mentioned include Cordova, J-Power, and a cooperative that bought Elwood units. The notes also say a controlling interest in one plant was sold to a sovereign fund. Check each name and fact before you use it.

Storms and cyber threats

Oct 13

Storm response is now a legislative issue. The notes say tornado counts have more than doubled from a past range of 20 to 35 a year, and restoration speed gets scrutiny. The plan is to treat severe weather as a lasting condition, not a one off. The tornado numbers need checking.

Oct 13

Cyber threats are expected to grow by 2027. Dane was set to attend a cybersecurity hearing on October 14 and report on its tone and standards. Power plants and municipal water systems were named as likely targets.

Go deeper, the terms

Background

Backstop procurement

An extra purchase of capacity made when the regular auction does not secure enough. The cost is shared among customers under set rules, so who pays is a key question.

Interruptibility

The right of a grid operator or utility to reduce a customer's power in a shortage. Customers usually get a lower price or a payment in return.

Voluntary versus involuntary

A voluntary program asks customers to sign up. An involuntary one cuts supply without a choice. The compensation question is much sharper for the second kind.

Administrative law judge

A hearing officer who reviews a case at a regulator and issues findings before the commission decides. Preliminary findings can shape the final order but do not decide it.

Mitigation plan and IRP

The mitigation plan lays out how to handle reliability effects of the CEJA retirement schedule. The IRP is a long range plan for meeting demand. They feed each other, so a data error in one can carry into the other.

Questions to ask
  • What exactly does the large load tariff say about interruptibility?
  • How will the 800 MW of Constellation upgrades be counted in the IRP?
  • Who pays for the PJM backstop purchase, and how is it split?
  • Which plants have extension rights today, and which do not?

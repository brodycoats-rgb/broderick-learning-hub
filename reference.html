Who decides what

A strong briefing sends each problem to the right decision maker. Start by separating the decision requested. A retail rate, a procurement requirement, a permit, a local approval, or a regional transmission question.

ActorRoleThe question to ask
General AssemblyWrites state law and assigns authorityWhat outcome should the statute require?
ICCRegulates utility rates, tariffs, and serviceWhat evidence and proceeding set reasonable terms?
IPAStatutory power procurement and planningHow does the proposal interact with procurement programs?
DCEORuns economic development programs, including the incentive programWhat agreement, eligibility, and performance conditions apply?
IEPA and water authoritiesEnvironmental permits and water obligationsWhich emissions, discharge, or water rules apply at this site?
Local governmentLand use, infrastructure, and local approvalsWhat will roads, water, emergency services, and neighbors experience?
MISO or PJMRegional operations, planning, markets, and transmissionWhat regional study or tariff governs this request?
FERCFederal jurisdiction over wholesale and interstate transmissionDoes the state proposal collide with a federal tariff?

An orientation map and not an exhaustive statement of jurisdiction. Verify the governing statute, tariff, and site before assigning responsibility.

Delivery and supply are differentIn Illinois the company delivering electricity and the entity supplying it can differ. A request that the delivery utility guarantee a customer's clean energy purchase is a different question from funding a substation.

PJM and MISO side by side

PJM, the capacity problem

  • Concern is a regional capacity shortage. Protect traditional customers in scarcity.
  • Tools are IRAS, BYONC, a Large Load Registry, backstop capacity, and forecast treatment.
  • Large load is 50 MW in the proposed definition.
  • Unsupported new load is reduced before traditional customers, implemented by utilities and states.
  • Illinois exposure is the ComEd zone and the northern cluster.

MISO, the integration problem

  • Concern is reliable technical integration across a fast growing footprint.
  • Tools are interconnection reliability requirements, a computational load category, forecasting, FLITS, and aligned load and generation studies.
  • Large load is more than 50 MW, with computational load at 25 MW of IT equipment or more.
  • Flexible or non firm service can be curtailed, and technical rules apply to all.
  • Illinois exposure is Ameren Illinois in Zone 4, central and southern Illinois.

PJM asks who brings the capacity. MISO asks whether the load will behave safely and whether anyone can see it. A statewide rule written for one can create problems in the other.

Eight pathways from one data center to the grid operator

  1. Forecast. Requests enter zonal and regional forecasts.
  2. Adequacy. Peak, reserve margin, accreditation, retirements.
  3. Transmission. Upgrades, deliverability, stability.
  4. Operations. Telemetry, ramps, restoration.
  5. Markets. Energy, capacity, congestion, uplift.
  6. Cost allocation. Who pays for facilities and for default.
  7. State retail. Retail tariffs and curtailment priority.
  8. Interregional. Seams, transfer limits, the Illinois split.

Policy fails when one pathway is fixed and another is ignored. Keep five problems apart. Energy, capacity, deliverability, performance in a disturbance, and cost allocation.

The project readiness ladder

Inquiry. Preliminary estimate and site geography.
Application. Site control, electrical data, deposit.
Study. Validated models, ramp schedule, backup plans.
Commitment. Agreement, collateral, minimum bill.
Construction. Milestones, permits, procurement.
Energization. Phased tests, telemetry, ramp checks.
Operation. Updates, incident reports, notice of expansion.

Missing evidence at a rung creates the risk the paper lists for that stage. Separate requested, studied, contracted, committed minimum, forecast peak, energized, and actual average megawatts. They answer different questions.

Do not carry a Missouri provision into IllinoisAmeren Missouri's Large Load Customer Service tariff applies at 75 MW or more and includes an 80 percent contract capacity minimum in billing demand. That is not an Illinois DS-4 requirement. Before January 2027 DS-4 distribution billing demand is the higher of on peak maximum kW or half the off peak maximum, and the posted tariff changes the basis to kVA in January 2027.

Thresholds differ in what they measure. Verify each tariff before external use. Pennsylvania uses threshold tests on aggregated nearby facilities. Wyoming uses dedicated procurement. Common features are separate rules for the largest loads, site control and ramp evidence, customer funded studies, minimum terms and collateral, exit fees, and state principles implemented through commission proceedings.

A 14 point checklist for any bill

  1. Definition. What facility, threshold, and status are covered?
  2. Authority. Which entity controls the decision today?
  3. Problem. What documented harm is it solving?
  4. Cost. Which cost, caused by whom, incurred when, recoverable how?
  5. Customer choice. Does it separate delivery from supply?
  6. Federal and regional boundary. Does it preserve FERC, MISO, PJM, and NERC authority?
  7. Readiness. Are site control, financing, tenants, permits, and ramp verified?
  8. Risk protection. Are deposits, collateral, minimums, and exit terms available?
  9. Environmental specificity. Are water source, consumption, emissions, noise, and location treated accurately?
  10. Confidentiality. Are trade secrets and critical infrastructure protected?
  11. Implementation. Are timeline, rulemaking, cure, variance, appeal, and enforcement workable?
  12. Duplication. Does another agency or bill already collect it?
  13. Equity. Are benefits and burdens measurable and mapped?
  14. Alternative. What amendment keeps the sponsor's goal with less risk?

Also ask a fifteenth question every time. Is this official expert feedback, a preliminary GR read, or a leadership approved position?

Glossary

MW and MWhPower at one moment against energy over time. 50 MW for 24 hours is 1,200 MWh.
IT loadPower used by the computers, as opposed to total facility load.
PUEPower usage effectiveness. Total facility energy divided by IT energy over the same period and boundary.
Large loadA customer whose scale or behavior creates unusual planning risk. PJM and MISO proposals use 50 MW in specified cases.
Computational loadMISO's subset of large load with at least 25 MW of IT equipment.
HyperscaleVery large campuses. There is no agreed megawatt line.
Load rampThe schedule by which a customer's demand grows after energization.
CapacityThe ability to meet demand under planning and market rules. Different from annual energy.
Resource adequacyEnough accredited supply and demand side capability to meet demand under a reliability standard.
DeliverabilityWhether the network can move capacity or energy to where it is needed.
Nameplate and firm MWRated, contract, peak, average, and firm megawatts are different measures.
Cost causationAssigning costs to the customer or activity that creates them.
Stranded costUnrecovered cost of facilities left underused. Count only the residual after reuse and recovery.
Financial assuranceCollateral or deposits protecting against default or exposure.
Minimum billing demandA billing floor that recovers fixed costs when actual demand is below reserved capacity.
Exit chargeA charge to recover prudent unrecovered costs when a customer ends service early.
Capacity reassignmentReleasing or transferring unused reserved capacity to another use.
TariffA filed schedule of rates and service rules.
RiderAdded provisions for a particular charge or service attached to a tariff.
DS-4Ameren Illinois Large General Delivery Service.
Rider EFCRecovers agreed facilities outside normal provision, with defined contribution and rental treatment.
ICCIllinois Commerce Commission. Regulates relevant utility rates, tariffs, and service.
IPAIllinois Power Agency. Statutory power procurement and planning.
DCEODepartment of Commerce and Economic Opportunity. Administers the incentive program.
IEPAIllinois Environmental Protection Agency. Environmental permits.
ARESAlternative retail electric supplier. Competitive supply under Illinois customer choice.
Customer choiceThe Illinois framework in which delivery and supply can come from different entities.
RTO or ISORegional entity that operates the high voltage grid, runs wholesale markets, coordinates adequacy, and plans transmission under FERC approved rules.
MISOMidcontinent Independent System Operator. Includes central and southern Illinois.
PJMRegional operator that includes northern Illinois and ComEd.
LRZ 4MISO Local Resource Zone 4, where Ameren Illinois sits.
FERCFederal Energy Regulatory Commission. Federal jurisdiction over wholesale and interstate transmission.
NERCNorth American Electric Reliability Corporation. Reliability standards authority.
FERC show cause orderThe June 18, 2026 orders requiring each of six RTOs and ISOs to justify or reform large load rules.
IRASPJM's proposed Interim Resource Adequacy Service. Unsupported new large load is reduced first in severe shortage.
BYONCBring your own new capacity. PJM's concept of supporting new demand with qualifying new capacity.
Large Load RegistryPJM tracker of large load size, location, timing, and backup capability.
FLITSMISO's Flexible Large load Interconnection and Transmission Service.
Load Addition Resource StudyMISO process that studies large load and associated generation together.
ERASMISO expedited review of qualifying generation and transmission projects.
IMMPJM's Independent Market Monitor.
Ride throughStaying connected or behaving in a controlled way through voltage or frequency disturbances.
Ramp rateHow fast load rises or falls, in MW per unit of time.
Firm serviceTransmission service backed by network capability and upgrades required under the tariff.
Flexible or non firm serviceEarlier or conditional service in exchange for curtailment under defined conditions.
Co-locationLoad located with or behind a generating facility.
Behind the meterGeneration on the customer's side of the meter.
Down regulationGeneration turning down in response to sudden load loss.
GrandfatheringNot applying a new framework to loads already approved. MISO reports roughly 30 GW as largely grandfathered.
AdditionalityWhether a clean energy commitment adds a new resource instead of relabeling existing supply.
Hourly matchingMatching clean energy to consumption hour by hour and not over a year.
Savings clauseBill language preserving federal, regional, or agency authority.
Withdrawal and consumptionWater taken from a source against the part not returned.
Closed loop coolingA cooling system that recirculates water instead of continually withdrawing and discharging.
BIPABiometric Information Privacy Act. Illinois law with a private right of action.
CEJAClimate and Equitable Jobs Act, Public Act 102-0662.
POWER ActThe Illinois bill package SB4016 and HB5513 for hyperscale data centers.
Vehicle billAny bill chosen to carry new language, often by amendment.
Shell billA placeholder bill with little real content, used later to carry new language.
Lame duck sessionA session after the election and before the new General Assembly is seated.
Veto sessionThe fall return of the General Assembly.
CBACommunity benefit agreement. A negotiated deal between a developer and a community.
Campus and buildingOne campus can hold many buildings, so counts can rise without new grid connections.
Trade secret and critical infrastructure protectionSafeguards that should accompany transparency rules for large campuses.

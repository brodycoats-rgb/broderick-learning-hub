Category deep dive — top 5

45 of 71 bills · 30 with SME feedback · most active: Data Privacy

Data Centers

14 bills · 20%

Position mix: 3 Oppose | 4 Neutral | 2 Pending | 5 open
Activity: 7 gatekeeping · 3 deadlines · SME feedback on 8 of 14

Primary concern is cumulative friction: tariffs, water/energy reporting, liability, prescriptive technology, and uncertain permitting could deter mobile large-load investment. Feedback repeatedly connects lost projects to weaker load growth and less fixed-cost dilution for customers.

Gas

10 bills · 14%

Position mix: 2 Support | 2 Oppose | 2 Neutral | 3 Pending | 1 mixed
Activity: 8 gatekeeping · 2 deadlines · SME feedback on 7 of 10

The category is strategically split. Support clusters around preserving fuel choice, reliability, and gas-asset value; opposition centers on appliance-labeling narratives. Future-of-Gas policy creates stranded-asset and customer-affordability concerns.

Energy Storage

10 bills · 14%

Position mix: 1 Support | 1 Oppose | 5 Neutral | 3 Pending
Activity: 8 gatekeeping · 1 deadline · 1 advanced · SME feedback on 6 of 10

Feedback balances reliability benefits against implementation risk. Siting bills are largely manageable, but financing mandates could stall procurements; storage-linked retirement flexibility may protect reliability and capacity prices.

Clean Energy Transition

6 bills · 8%

Position mix: 5 Neutral | 1 Pending
Activity: 5 gatekeeping · 1 deadline · SME feedback on 5 of 6

Most proposals have limited direct utility impact and are treated neutrally. The meaningful policy issue is transition pace: slower targets may ease resource-adequacy and near-term cost pressure, while new databases add paperwork without clear benefit.

Data Privacy

5 bills · 7%

Position mix: 1 Oppose | 3 Neutral | 1 Pending
Activity: 1 gatekeeping · 3 deadlines · 1 advanced · SME feedback on 4 of 5

Privacy protections are supported in principle, but broad frameworks could duplicate existing utility safeguards, require major technology and staffing investment, disrupt assistance-program records, and ultimately shift compliance cost to customers.

Practical policy takeaway
Pritzker paused new data-center incentives in 2026 and wants large loads to bring their own energy resources and protect customer costs. Bailey wants data centers to fund customer relief and favors rolling back portions of CEJA to retain conventional generation. Corbett has raised concerns about the scale of data-center growth.

Bundling into the data center bill

Senate leadership says to hold technical items and attach them to a broad data center energy bill.

More on Legislative strategy

What was said

The organization behind legislative tracking is restructuring. A speaker said only limited updates are happening while that group restructures.

Utilities have negotiated tactical items. The speaker described changes to procedure, mostly tactical, negotiated among the companies.

Sign ups matter. The package moves as long as companies sign up, and the notes say NRG has. Ohio stakeholders were also mentioned.

Senate leadership advised patience. The advice was to keep powder dry and wait for a better climate to move.

The plan is to bundle. The speaker said to get an energy bill together on data centers and then put the technical items on it. The decision was to pause standalone tactical amendments and attach them to the broad data center energy bill.

Why it matters

My reading

A small technical fix is easy to lose on its own. A large bill is a vehicle that is more likely to move, but it ties your fix to the fate of the large bill. The next day's meeting questioned whether a comprehensive data center bill will actually move, which is why this plan needs a second look.

Say it in plain words

Senate leadership advised us to hold our technical utility items and attach them to a larger data center energy bill. NRG and Ohio participants are signing on. The upside is that a big bill has momentum. The risk, which came up the next day, is that the big bill may not happen. So the plan needs a sponsor, a list of what goes in, and a fallback.

Vehicle bills

A vehicle is a bill that already has momentum and is used to carry other provisions. Attaching an item can help, but opponents of the vehicle then become opponents of your item. Weigh that before you commit.

State House races

Open seats and rematches in the suburbs and near Danville. The net change is one or two seats or a wash.

More on Elections

What was said

Primary defeats reshaped some seats. Speaker 1 noted Rep. Jaime Andrade lost a primary to a DSA backed challenger supported by Senator Cruz in Avondale.

District 47 is an open seat. Rep. Amy Grant is retiring. Speaker 2 projected a Democratic pickup, since the Democratic candidate has unified backing while Republican support for the other candidate has lagged.

District 52 is a rematch. Rep. Marty McLaughlin faces Maria Peterson in a race decided by about 50 votes last time. Democrats are running aggressive negative TV ads against McLaughlin.

Two incumbents are being defended. Speaker 2 assessed that the Democratic baseline will protect Amy Kroll's seat despite controversy, and that Jessica Dixon Heitman (the notes write Heidman) will keep hers after launching TV next week.

Danville area, Schweizer against Roberson. Rep. Schweizer won narrowly in 2024. Democrats gave Roberson heavy TV and mail support while Republicans leaned on digital ads. Speaker 2 tilted it toward a Democratic pickup.

Seat counts were loose. The group said “Senate two, House three”, and Speaker 1 said “net one.” Speaker 2 judged that House Democrats may gain one or two seats or finish about even. The phrases “seven plus” and “seven plus three” are unclear.

Why it matters

My reading

Even a gain of one or two House seats changes committee math. More important for utilities, a bigger majority is not an easier majority. Members of the same party disagree about energy costs, environmental rules and labor, so legislation still has to be built member by member.

Why TV against digital matters

Television reaches many people but costs a lot. Digital is cheaper and more targeted. When one side buys heavily on TV and the other relies on digital, the first often gets broader visibility, which the notes see in Danville and Barrington.

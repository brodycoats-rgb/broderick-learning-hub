In plain language

The slide opens with the national mood. Fewer than three in ten Americans believe the news media report accurately and fairly, down from four in ten five years ago. People's average confidence in 14 major institutions has stayed under 30 percent for three years in a row. Roughly three out of four people name the cost of living as their top economic worry.

Put together, the slide says people begin from doubt. Any organization that asks for more money, a utility included, is heard through that doubt, and rising bills make it worse.

Intent

This is the setup slide for everything that follows. It tells the team that a technical case for the grid plan will not land on its own, because lawmakers and customers hear every investment as a cost first. The three cards turn that into a working test for any message. Does it tie a charge to a specific result people can see in reliability, capacity or service? Does it show restraint with real examples of costs avoided or managed? Does it say plainly who pays, how regulators weigh the cost, and how households are protected when large customers shift costs?

That test lines up with the Attorney General's question about what utilities do on affordability beyond comparing prices with other states, and with the affordability fact sheet due Nov 6.

How to use it in a conversation

  • Lead with the outcome a legislator can picture, such as fewer or shorter outages, before the dollar figure.
  • Bring one concrete example of cost restraint rather than a general claim.
  • Be ready to explain the large load protections when a data center comes up.

In plain language

The company's own customer survey shows trust going up this year after sliding in 2025. The net trust score is positive ratings minus negative ratings, so +24 means about 24 more points of favorable than unfavorable. It moved from 22 to 24 between the two most recent survey waves, which is the 9 percent improvement on the slide, and it now sits 7 points above the peer group of other utilities.

The strongest trust factors are capability and reliability, meaning customers believe the company can keep the lights on. The weakest are humanity, meaning caring about customers as people, and transparency, meaning being open about costs and decisions.

Intent

The slide gives the team good news and a limit on it. It shows that reliability work is earning credit, which supports the grid plan argument, and it shows where credit is thin. Affordability, humanity and transparency are the same weak spots slide 9 says the company must address. The takeaway is to keep making the reliability case while adding more plain talk about cost and who is protected.

It also explains the meeting comment that trust on reliability is high while trust on cost related issues is low.

In plain language

The top half tracks the size of the capital plan, which is money spent on poles, wires, substations and similar equipment that goes into service during 2028 to 2031. Each marker is one point in the case. The company first filed $2.65B. Later rounds of testimony nudged it to $2.69B in the surrebuttal and $2.75B in the supplemental version. Three optional add on programs, called IGP2A, IGP2B and IGP2C, would raise it step by step to $2.84B, $3.09B and finally $3.11B.

The Administrative Law Judge's proposed order lands at $2.48B, which is below the filed amount but not far below. The marker labeled blue chip, $2.35B, is the internal mark for a favorable result.

The bottom half shows operating and maintenance spending, the day to day expense side. It climbs from $880M in the direct testimony to about $1.0B in the surrebuttal, and the slide attributes the increase mostly to new beneficial electrification programs, such as ChargeReady, that the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act requires.

Intent

The slide answers the question of where the case stands. It lets leadership see how the investment number has moved, how much of the original ask the ALJ kept, and how much room remains between the proposed order and the internal favorable mark. That is why Betsey said there is not much left for the Commission to trim between the proposed order and the blue chip figure.

It also explains why the update to $3.11B was a deliberate choice. The extra dollars were offered as optional programs, partly to address reversing reliability trends and add distributed energy resource integration, and with the expectation that some of it would be cut. The slide also separates capital from O&M so that growth in expense is not mistaken for growth in the grid plan itself.

How the three slides fit together

Slide 9 says people start from doubt about cost. Slide 10 says the company has earned credit on reliability this year but not on cost or openness. Slide 13 shows the dollar story the team has to explain, where the plan started, where the ALJ landed and how much expense growth comes from required programs. Read in that order, the deck argues that the investment case has to be told through outcomes customers value, with plain talk about price and protection.

The practical use is the non privileged talking points due Oct 28. They need to carry the message of slides 9 and 10 and the reliability consequence from slide 13 without the dollar detail or the internal mark.

Venice engagement strategy

The group agreed to engage Venice now, starting low key, with a short plan due October 21.

More on Reliability and retirements

What was said

The group agreed to engage Venice now. An external affairs call on October 13 led participants to agree it is time to actively engage the Venice community about the plant.

It starts as soft engagement. The approach must avoid signaling issues that could cause political blowback right around the elections. Speaker 2 is tasked with building an engagement strategy from the community's point of view.

The first outreach goes to an investor. The initial target is a local investor heavily involved in economic development, with the goal of later engaging municipal leaders about the loss of the tax base.

Work from the law as written. Some speculate that a different administration might change the retirement mandates, but the consensus is to work from current law.

Closures may happen regardless. Speaker 1 said several generators have spent years executing closure plans and would likely close even if late legislation changed.

A short plan is due. Speaker 2 will draft a brief, non technical plan about community impact by October 21.

Why it matters

My reading

Local officials who hear about a tax loss for the first time from a news story feel blindsided. Starting quietly with a trusted local voice gives time for facts to land, while the election calendar is respected.

Say it in plain words

The group agreed that it is time to engage the Venice community, starting low key with the election calendar in mind. The first contact is a local investor involved in economic development, and later municipal leaders. The plan works from the law as written, and Speaker 2 will draft a short, plain language plan for October 21 focused on community impact.

What soft engagement can look like

Share facts and listen first. Offer simple, plain information on the tax effect. Do not ask for anything or take a public position. Keep a record of who you spoke with and what they asked.

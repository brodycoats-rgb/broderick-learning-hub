PJM, MISO, and affordability

Both parties pressure PJM and Governor Shapiro secured price caps. Commission seats and committee chair talk are unconfirmed.

More on PJM, MISO, affordability

What was said

Both parties are pressing PJM. One speaker said the Trump administration is all over PJM about prices, and Democratic governors are too, though their fixes differ between more thermal capacity and more renewables. PJM is also a convenient scapegoat for rising bills.

Two governors are talking. Governor Shapiro of Pennsylvania and Governor Pritzker are discussing affordability and data center load growth. Shapiro secured limits on PJM capacity prices, while MISO lacks comparable mechanics.

Executive staff are frustrated. Gubernatorial staff were upset with a utility for not addressing the problem. The notes name Exxon, which is likely a slip for Exelon. It is unclear whether the pushback is substance or theater.

Personnel to watch. Rep. Joyce Mason may become a committee chair and plans to advance a long stalled bill transcribed as Archie Ovody. Commission turnover includes Reddick's expiring term and seats held by Paradis and McKay.

Public anger is visible. At a town hall with Rep. Ann Williams and the Clean Jobs Coalition, a constituent accused her of fronting for multi billion dollar corporations, and organizers cut off public comment. Williams answered that she is not running for anybody. This exchange is unverified.

Why it matters

My reading

Affordability has become the political frame for energy. Utilities will be asked to explain why bills move and what they can do about it, and the answer differs between PJM and MISO. People moves at the commission and in committees will shape how those questions are decided.

Say it in plain words

Electricity prices now worry both parties. Pennsylvania's governor got limits on PJM capacity prices, MISO has no equivalent, and Illinois leaders are talking about what they can do. Executive staff are unhappy with utilities, and public anger is visible at town halls. Several people may be moving, including a possible new committee chair and commission seats up for renewal, but the names are unconfirmed.

Why a price cap is a tradeoff

A cap holds down what customers pay in the short term but can weaken the signal that tells developers to build. The first calculator below lets you see both effects at once.

Why the bill is not one number

A bill has distribution, supply, transmission, capacity and program costs. A move in any one can change the total. A good answer to an angry customer names the piece that moved.

Terms on this page

PJM Background
The regional grid operator that covers the ComEd area.
MISO Background
The regional grid operator for much of the Midwest, including the Ameren Illinois area.
Capacity market From the meetings
A market that pays resources to commit to being available. Those costs flow into supply charges.
FERC Background
The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission. It oversees many wholesale electricity market rules.

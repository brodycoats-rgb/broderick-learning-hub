The five big ideas to carry into any room

  1. Reliability is now a political story, not only an engineering one.
  2. Large new load and shrinking firm supply are colliding at the same time.
  3. Where authority sits (legislature, ICC, IEPA, RTO) decides what is possible.
  4. Timing and sequencing matter as much as substance, especially around the veto session.
  5. Plain, fact based messaging beats strategy heavy messaging with a general audience.

Mixed self check, ten questions

Which issue is first in the priority ranking and why?

Capacity, retirements and the IRP, because about 3 GW may leave without firm replacement and the IRP is expected right before veto session.

What did leadership advise about small technical utility bills?

Hold them and attach them to a broader data center energy bill.

When are final ICC grid plan orders expected?

By December 2026.

What does the ICC gas report do?

It gives advice. It is not a binding phase out.

What are the two legal risks in the siting case?

Loss of state preemption, and the special legislation problem for tax exemptions.

Which two RTOs serve Illinois?

PJM in the north and MISO in the center and south.

Why do plant closures matter to local governments?

They lose a large property tax payer.

What is the PIPP cap for a household with one utility?

6 percent of income, as summarized.

Which group is described as likely to ask for unemployment benefits during strikes?

Organized labor.

Why is Senate first the suggested path?

The Senate has been steadier than the House, and early coalition work keeps messaging disciplined.

Grid operators and prices

Most of Illinois buys power through one of two regional grid operators. Their rules shape what generators are paid and what customers see on a bill.

Two operators, two problems

A regional transmission organization runs the grid and the wholesale market across several states. Northern Illinois is served by one operator and central and southern Illinois by another. They have different market designs and different supply and demand pictures, so a fix that works in one may not work in the other.

Capacity markets and price caps

A capacity market pays resources to commit to being available at the hardest moment. When supply gets tight, the auction price rises, and that cost moves into customer bills. A price cap limits how high the auction can clear. A cap spares customers in the short run, and it also weakens the signal that encourages new plants to be built.

Neither choice is free. Lower prices now can mean less supply later, and higher prices now can mean customers pay for resources that arrive slowly.

What is inside a bill

A bill has many parts, including supply, delivery, taxes and fees, and program charges. A rise in one part does not mean the others rose. Good communication starts by showing which part changed and why.

Questions worth asking

  • Which operator serves this customer, and what are its auction results?
  • How much of a bill increase comes from supply, and how much from delivery?
  • Who gains and who loses from a price cap?

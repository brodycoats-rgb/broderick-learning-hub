Missouri planning call and regional effect

Keeping Venice open was said to save Ameren Missouri customers a large amount, which needs checking. A maximum generation event occurred October 13.

More on Reliability and retirements

What was said

A Missouri planning call took place on October 12. A presenter went through generation resources and coal retirement charts. Speaker 2 asked how changes to Venice under the Illinois IRP would affect the Missouri IRP and long term sustainability goals.

Keeping Venice open was said to save Missouri customers money. The presenter said it saves Ameren Missouri customers hundreds of millions of dollars by avoiding new infrastructure or purchased market power to meet peak demand.

Replacement supply is years away. A colleague indicated that enough replacement inventory will not be built until at least 2035.

Venice is a buffer supply. The plant acts as a buffer for the MISO Zone 4 overlap.

A maximum generation event happened on October 13. Peaking plants reportedly kept the grid online. Speaker 1 wants to know how power was split between Missouri and Illinois during such events.

Control the message in Illinois. The decision was that Illinois communications should focus on MISO reliability and not discuss savings for Missouri customers, with the Zone 4 overlap as the main reliability rationale.

Why it matters

My reading

The regional benefit is real if the number holds up, but it can be politically unhelpful when told to Illinois audiences. That is why the notes call for keeping the Illinois message about reliability. The distribution during a maximum generation event is the key unknown.

Say it in plain words

On the Missouri call we heard that keeping Venice open may save Ameren Missouri customers hundreds of millions of dollars, that replacement supply will not be fully built until at least 2035, and that Venice is a buffer for the MISO Zone 4 overlap. A maximum generation event on October 13 reportedly relied on peaking plants. In Illinois, we talk about reliability and keep the Missouri savings out of the message. We still need to find the Missouri to Illinois split during those events.

What a maximum generation event is

The grid operator calls on every available generator when supply is very tight. Peaking plants, which run only at such times, are the ones that often make the difference. That is the argument for documenting actual emergency use.

Terms on this page

Maximum generation event From the meetings
A time when the grid operator asks for all available generation. Peaking plants were reported to help on October 13.
MISO Background
The regional grid operator for much of the Midwest, including the Ameren Illinois area.
Peaking plant From the meetings
A plant built to run during short periods of very high demand or when other units or lines are out.
IRP Background
Integrated Resource Plan. A long range plan for meeting demand with supply. The notes expect IRP findings in November.

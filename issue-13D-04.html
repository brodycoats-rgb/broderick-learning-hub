Authority to mandate procurement or tariffs

The speaker doubted the IRP lets anyone order purchases and opposes interruptibility and facility tariffs.

More on Plans and regulation

What was said

The speaker weighed who can order a purchase. One line says the state can require certain things but cannot simply tell a customer to buy power from a particular source.

The IRP question was asked directly. Asked whether the IRP gives authority to mandate this at a regulatory level, Speaker 1 said they think the answer would be no.

The commission can stop things. The speaker said the commission has clear authority to stop a request.

The speaker opposes two kinds of tariffs. They said they have opposed tariffs for interruptibility and tariffs for basic facilities, and do not think they are needed. The notes treat this as an indicative position.

Why it matters

My reading

Whether a plan can compel purchases decides if a gap can be closed by order or only by incentive. If the answer is incentive, then price signals and contracts matter more. The speaker's position also shapes which tariff ideas the group will push back on.

Say it in plain words

Who has the power to order a purchase? The speaker doubts that the resource plan gives anyone that authority, and noted that the commission clearly can stop a request. The speaker also said they oppose tariffs for interruptibility and tariffs for basic facilities. That is one person's view, not a company decision. A short legal memo on the limits of IRP and commission authority would settle the fact question.

Mandate, incentive or contract

A mandate says you must buy. An incentive says you are paid more if you do. A contract says two parties agreed. Each has a different legal basis and a different political cost, so the first question about any plan should be which one is being proposed.

Interruptible tariff From the meetings
A rate that lets a utility reduce a customer's supply under set conditions, usually in return for a lower price.

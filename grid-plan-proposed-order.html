Read this first

This is a proposed order. It is the administrative law judges' recommendation to the Commission, and it is not final until the Commissioners vote. Parties can still file exceptions. Describe it as a positive recommendation with targeted adjustments, never as a final win or loss.

Bottom line

Strategy intact, projects tested one by one

The proposed order supports the core of the plan and asks for stronger project-level proof where the record fell short. It also lands on both sides of the company's wish list, so the honest summary is mixed and mostly positive.

Supported

  • Approval of the 2028 to 2031 Grid Plan with modifications
  • The cost-effectiveness framework, ADMS and DERMS
  • Reactive hosting capacity
  • All three EV programs
  • Asset Management Excellence, Economic Development Pilot and LiDAR/OptoAI
  • Statutory compliance on all five topics reviewed

Reduced or conditioned

  • Investment adjustments that generally track Staff
  • New Business, down by $9.4M
  • Underground Redesign, reduced
  • DER grid strengthening, approved in direction and settled in the 2027 rate proceedings
  • Beneficial Electrification, pending IT cost information in the compliance filing

Not supported

  • Reversing the Reliability Trend (public analysis cites up to $400M of additional funding)
  • Proactive hosting capacity funding
  • Subtransmission Line Resiliency, eliminated
  • General Pilot Programs portfolio
  • Several Staff-aligned reductions to the baseline plan (see the numbers section)
Process and dates

The order moves through exceptions, replies and a Commission vote. Dates below come from the company's regulatory team and the public analysis. The compliance filing date is still to be confirmed.

  7. January 2028
    The 2028 to 2031 plan period begins.

EV math to word carefully

Government Relations

How to talk about it

The framing that holds up best is balanced. The order backs the core plan, and it shows the review process working by trimming what needed more evidence.

Supportive

The proposed order backs the core modernization strategy of reliability, grid readiness, EV support and equity.

Disciplined

It trims projects that needed more evidence. That is the review working, and it is not a rejection of the plan.

Forward-looking

Future proposals need clear needs, alternatives and customer benefits, supported by Ameren-specific data.

The one-line frame

"The ALJs recommended approval with modifications, and the Commission's final decision is expected in December."

Safe to say

  • The proposed order supports the core plan while making targeted adjustments.
  • The Commission has not voted. Exceptions are due October 21 and replies November 4.
  • The order reviews investments project by project instead of using a blanket cap.
  • Bills are projected to grow about 2.3 percent a year, below historical inflation.

Avoid

  • "We won" or "we lost."
  • Calling the order final, or the dollar totals settled.
  • Treating the $400M and the $212M as one number.
  • Promising a customer bill impact beyond the 2.3 percent projection.
  • Suggesting the order rejects innovation. It supported well-defined pilots.
45-second talk track

"The proposed order is generally supportive of our 2028 through 2031 Grid Plan. The judges recommend approval with modifications. It backs grid modernization, reliability, readiness for distributed energy and our EV programs, and it finds the plan compliant with the statute on cost-effectiveness, equity, affordability, long-term investment and beneficial electrification. It also trims or conditions items where the judges wanted more evidence, like additional reliability trend funding, proactive hosting capacity and the general pilot pool. It is not final. Parties file exceptions this month and next, and we expect the Commission to act in December. The core strategy holds, and strong project-level justification will matter even more going forward."

Thirty-second version for a legislator

"The judges looked at our grid plan and recommended approving it with some changes. They want more proof on certain reliability spending and they did not fund upgrades built ahead of solar demand, but they backed the core plan and the customer programs. Bills are projected to grow slower than inflation. The Commission decides in December."

Practice

Tough questions and answers

"Didn't the ICC cut $400 million from your plan?"

That was additional Reliability Trend funding Ameren requested, and the proposed order did not approve it because it had not been vetted project by project. The core reliability work moves forward. It is a request for more detail and not a rejection of the need.

"Why should customers pay for this?"

Bills are projected to grow about 2.3 percent a year, below historical inflation. Ameren projects that nearly half the benefits will reach low-income and equity communities, and Rider ESDA targets bill help to those who need it most.

"Why not just cap spending?"

The judges considered a 7 percent cap and chose project-by-project review instead. That way the Commission can keep what is needed and cut what is not justified.

"Does this weaken reliability?"

The order supports reliability investment, including about $36.9 million for selected 4kV-to-12kV conversions and $49.3 million in automation. It eliminated Subtransmission Line Resiliency, reduced Underground Redesign and wants larger projects backed by Ameren-specific data and alternatives analysis.

"Are solar customers being shortchanged?"

The order supports upgrades when real interconnection requests come in. It also asks DER owners to pay a fair share, with final allocation in the 2027 rate proceedings. Upgrades built ahead of expected growth were not funded, and that is the point solar advocates are most likely to raise.

"Why did your pilots get rejected?"

Pilots with clear plans, like LiDAR/OptoAI and the Economic Development Pilot, were approved. The general pilot funding pool needs more detail, and there is a path to bring it back. Ameren withdrew the Hydrogen Pilot itself.

"Is this final?"

No. It is a proposed order. Parties file exceptions on October 21 and replies on November 4, and then the Commission issues its final decision, which the company expects in December.

"Why was Reversing the Reliability Trend turned down?"

The public analysis says the additional funding had not been adequately vetted. The company is still reviewing the judges' full reasoning, so say only that the order asked for stronger project-level support, and hold anything more until the regulatory team finishes its review.

Proposed order

Brief on exceptions (BOE)

A party's written argument asking the Commission to change the proposed order. Due October 21, 2026.

Reply to exceptions

A party's response to the other side's exceptions. Due November 4, 2026.

Compliance filing

The updated Grid Plan Ameren files after the final order, due 50 or more days later. Expected around February 2027.

Right-sizing

Trimming budgets to what the record supports. The judges adopted Staff's approach, and about 80 percent of the adjustments focus on corrective maintenance.

Plant in service (PIS)

The capital cost of equipment that is built and in use. The company's preliminary adjustment total is about $212M of PIS plus $15M of O&M.

Reversing the Reliability Trend

The additional reliability program the proposed order did not support. Public analysis cites up to $400M of additional funding.

Asset Management Excellence

Additive program of about $20M that the proposed order approved.

Alternatives analysis

A showing of the other options considered. Required for future Category 3 projects over $3M.

Reactive versus proactive hosting capacity

Reactive upgrades respond to real DER requests and were approved. Proactive upgrades are built ahead of expected growth and were not funded.

DER grid strengthening

Directionally approved. DER owners should bear a fair share under a Massachusetts-style allocation, with final decisions in the 2027 rate proceedings.

Rider ESDA

Bill support for eligible customers that targets about a 3 percent energy burden. Set to launch in October 2026.

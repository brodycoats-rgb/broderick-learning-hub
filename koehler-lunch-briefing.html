Part 2 Back to topThe lunch from start to finish

Before Monday. Check with Ameren compliance on meal and reporting rules for state officials, including who pays. This moved up from the last line of this part.

You have known the Senator for 16 years, so keep this natural. Treat the stages as a map and not a script, and follow his lead on formality and pace.

  1. 1

    Greeting first 5 to 10 minutes

    • Arrive 10 minutes early and pick a table where you can talk easily. Phone away, papers in your bag until the end.
    • Greet him the way you always have. After 16 years, warmth and a bit of shared history will do more than formality.
    • Let him steer the ordering. Choose something easy to eat, since you will be doing some of the talking.
  2. 2

    Icebreakers and family next 10 minutes

    • Ask about his family by name and follow up on something specific from your last conversation. Fill in your notes on the field card before you go.
    • Bridge lightly to the fall. "How is the season treating you? What are you hearing back home, and are people mentioning energy bills?"
    • Note what he says about bills and veto session, because it tells you where to lean later.
  3. 3

    Peoria and district conversation about 10 minutes, before your pitch

    • Pick two or three topics from Parts 3 and 4, starting with the least contentious, such as storm recovery, Rivian, and the Peoria Solar Energy Center.
    • Ask, then listen. The goal is to learn what he is thinking about his own community so your briefing lands in his world.
    • Bridge when there is a natural opening. "That connects to something in front of the ICC this fall, if you are open to hearing it."
  4. 4

    Your briefing about 10 minutes, with pauses

    • Use the 5 minute track in Part 6, delivered in chunks, and pause after each of the three messages.
    • Say early that you have no ask. "I am not here to ask for anything. I wanted you to hear it from us, and I would value your honest read."
    • Mention that Phase I defines scope and benefits and that cost and customer impact come in Phase II starting January 2027.
  5. 5

    Questions and feedback 15 to 20 minutes

    • Hand it to him cleanly. "That is the overview. What is your honest read?"
    • Choose two or three feedback questions from Part 7. With a 16 year relationship you can be blunt, for example "what is the weakest part of this?"
    • Take brief notes, do not defend, and say so when you do not know something and promise a follow up.
  6. 6

    Closing and next steps last 5 to 10 minutes

    • Play back what you heard from him in a sentence or two.
    • Offer concrete follow ups such as the Peoria and McLean area reliability numbers this week and a note when the Mitigation Plan draft is out on October 8.
    • Hand over the one pager and your card with your cell number, thank him, and end on something personal.
    • Send a short thank you within 24 hours that mentions one thing he said. A draft is on the field card.

He is engaged on storm recovery and his district took the worst of it, so expect this topic. Use the facts below and promise specifics when you lack them. Do not guess at restoration times or costs.

  • The August 13 storm brought gusts near 70 mph. Ameren outages peaked around 26,000, and Bloomington-Normal was the most severely affected area, with outages also in Peoria, Champaign, and Danville.
  • Sub transmission poles, distribution poles, and transmission structures were down in McLean County, and Ameren brought in contractors and crews from other regions.
  • Across all utilities about 29,000 customers lost power. The Governor's FEMA request covers storms from August 11 to 16 in eight counties, including Cook and Will, and McLean County reported $42.5 million in damage. That damage figure is not isolated to outage damage, so keep it separate from the outage story.
  • OSF St. Joseph Medical Center diverted its emergency room after flooding and roof damage, and Connect Transit suspended service. The report ties the hospital to flooding and roof damage, so do not attribute it to the outage.
  • Rivian's plant was hit by an EF-1 tornado on April 17 and restarted production within five days.

Hardest questions to rehearse

Say each answer out loud once. New and changed answers are marked. Confirm the new wording with your regulatory team before you use it.

Is this rate base growth presented as reliability?

That is a fair question. The ICC reviews every part of the plan, and we are accountable to reliability measures with financial consequences. Right now the ICC is reviewing need and scope. Cost and customer impact are decided in Phase II beginning January 2027, where they get tested. I am glad to walk through how any specific investment is justified.

Know this before he asks. A February 2026 report says Ameren projects about 6.2 percent annual electric sales growth through 2030 and a $31.8 billion five year investment plan. Both figures are Ameren company-wide, not just Illinois, and they are exactly what a rate base question would use. Do not volunteer them. Know the Illinois share if he asks. Source 25 News Ameren
CUB says more than $300 million of this plan is wasteful or questionable. What do you say?

That is what the hearing process is for. CUB, ICC Staff, and others test every line, and the judges weigh it. I will not argue the individual items over lunch, but I am glad to walk you through how any one of them is justified, and I would like to hear whether any of them concern you.

Know the six items. Reliability metrics ($125 million), customer growth ($78.4 million), the proactive economic development fund ($44.1 million), reliability projects under $100,000 ($59.2 million), the hydrogen pilot ($20.3 million), and pole replacement ($14.7 million). CUB argues the growth forecast is overstated when system load is forecast to decline. Confirm wording

Sources CUB, June 29 · CUB, August 3

Bills are already too high. Why more?

Much of the recent increase came from regional supply and capacity costs, while this plan addresses local delivery. It will have a customer cost, and that is set in Phase II beginning January 2027, so nothing is decided on price yet. The value is fewer outages and less emergency work later.

Be ready for him to connect this to the delivery side. Ameren has a pending $65.3 million reconciliation request (Docket 26-0214) that CUB says includes at least $14.8 million in overcharges. Acknowledge that delivery costs are part of his constituents' bills too.

What will this cost my constituents?

The plan as filed is about $2.8 billion over four years. Confirm Under the ICC process, this phase defines the projects, scope, and benefits. Cost and the cost to customers are decided in Phase II beginning January 2027. I will make sure you get the numbers as they are available.

This replaces "we are still working on the total," which would not hold up with the filed figure public.

Where does Ameren stand on the POWER Act and data centers?

Check with the team before Monday. A safe framing is that data centers are a major source of new load and the grid has to serve it reliably, the state is deciding the rules, and you would like to hear his view. Do not state a company position you have not confirmed.

Why not wait a year?

The need keeps growing, and delay can mean more emergency repairs and less flexibility on cost. The better question is whether the work is necessary and paced responsibly.

Can't clean energy solve this?

It is part of the answer, but every project still connects to the distribution system. As more power flows both ways, the grid needs upgrades to handle it safely and keep new connections moving.

What does this do for Peoria and McLean County?

This is where you need district figures in hand. Without them, say you will send him the Peoria and McLean area reliability and planned work numbers this week.

Where it stands

Illinois is no longer debating whether data centers need guardrails. It is debating what the rules will be. The likely path is a negotiated package that starts from the Governor's seven principles and the POWER Act and borrows language from narrower bills.

  1. Feb 6 POWER Act filed as SB4016 and HB5513.
  2. Feb 18 Governor proposes a two year pause on data center tax credits.
  3. April House and Senate hearings on local impacts, energy, and water.
  4. May 31 Spring session ends with no movement on the POWER Act.
  5. June 5 Governor pauses new incentive agreements and issues seven principles.
  6. Summer Working group meets. Governor's team and labor circulate frameworks.
  7. Sept 24 to 28 Governor calls for November action and a compromise bill.
  8. Nov 17 to 19, Dec 1 to 3 Veto session. Environmental lobby day expected November 18.
  9. Early January Possible lame duck session before the new General Assembly is seated.

Source is the knowledge center timeline, October 8, 2026. Most spring bills were re-referred to committees instead of being defeated, which keeps them available until the 104th General Assembly ends in January 2027.

The Governor's seven principles

Issued June 5, 2026 with the incentive pause. It is the closest thing to an administration outline, and our read is that it is the floor of any package.

What the Governor has said since

  • July 21. Urged passage of the POWER Act, said data centers should bring their own clean energy, and criticized regional grid structures for driving up costs. He said data centers are not the sole cause of higher bills.
  • August. Said the state was not talking about AI hyperscalers when the 2019 incentives passed, and that the goal is to keep electric and water rates from rising because of data centers.
  • September 24. Said the General Assembly will consider the POWER Act in November, with three priorities. Bring dollars and energy, bring closed loop water systems, and give communities transparency and a local say. A compromise was the goal.
  • September 25. Said he is not pursuing a statewide moratorium. Later said the state does not want to block data centers as long as they follow the POWER Act's rules.

Why labor is not a side issueUnion opposition helped sink data center regulatory bills this spring (E&E News, June 1). IUOE Local 150 pushed back on the incentive pause. The fight over bring your own power is not simply clean against fossil. Labor and some legislators want data centers to be able to bring their own gas.

How the pieces fit

Core vehicle

HB5513 and SB4016, the POWER Act framework.

Likely building blocks

SB2181 reporting, SB3761 self direct, SB4004 and SB3120 water, HB5818 local control.

Negotiating pressure

SB3830 tariff mandates, HB4172 assurance limits, biometric privacy, and what counts as eligible supply.

Outside the core

HB4319 property, SB3578 ownership, SB3030 crypto noise, HB4989 cooling.

Say it plainlyThe bill list is best understood as a parts catalog. The POWER Act is the main framework, and the narrower bills provide language on reporting, water, tariffs, supply, incentives, and local control. We watch which pieces get pulled into the final vehicle, not just whether each standalone bill moves.

Votes and timing

71 and 36

Three fifths in the House and Senate. Needed in veto session for any bill that should take effect right away.

60 and 30

A simple majority of members elected. Enough in a January lame duck session, even for an immediate effective date.

1 hour

The shortest notice seen for a committee hearing during the push. The Constitution says only reasonable public notice.

That math is why some expect final action to slide to January. Scrutiny of House leadership and the November 3 election could also affect timing. Sponsors have talked about attaching POWER Act language to a vehicle bill, so the number you are watching may never carry the final text.

Outside drafts to know about

In September, Greater St. Louis, Inc. shared an initial draft, the Data Center Accountability and Grid Reliability Act, as a 2027 legislative priority and asked Ameren for feedback. It tracks the Governor's framework closely. It includes an incentive pause, cost causation and a data center rate class at the ICC, a renewable support obligation, quarterly water reporting, air standards for backup generators, host community agreements, and an interruptible service concept.

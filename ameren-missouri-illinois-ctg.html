Start here

Core idea. These plants are not designed to run every day. They are designed to be available, start quickly, and produce power when demand is high, another resource is out, imports are constrained, or MISO declares an emergency. Their value is output plus readiness.

What you will be able to do

  • Explain a CTG in plain words, with the jet-engine analogy and the fire-station analogy plus its limit.
  • Distinguish MW, MWh, nameplate, accredited capacity, capacity factor and run hours, and label the basis of every number.
  • Describe the five plants, including why Pinckneyville is different and why Venice is first.
  • State emissions honestly, and explain how CEJA creates the clock without calling the reliability provision automatic or permanent.
  • Apply the retirement readiness test and tax-base exposure framing without overclaiming.
  • Deliver a 30-second, 2-minute and 5-minute version, a 12-slide briefing, and answer skeptical questions.

Learning path

Eight modules. Open each, try the retrieval prompt, then read.

1The machineWhat a CTG is and how it makes power

A jet engine that makes electricity

Retrieve first. Before you read on, say the five steps from air intake to electricity out loud in under 20 seconds.

A combustion turbine generator (CTG) is a large jet engine whose useful output is a spinning shaft, not thrust. Air is pulled in and compressed. Fuel is burned in the combustor. The hot gas spins turbine blades. The shaft turns a generator, and a transformer and switchyard step the voltage up and connect to the grid.

The five Illinois plants are discussed as simple-cycle units. Exhaust heat is not routed through a steam system. That makes starts faster and operation simpler, and it means more fuel per MWh than a combined-cycle plant. Efficiency is not the same as reliability value: one measures fuel use, the other measures availability when the grid needs it.

Anchor. Jet engine plus a generator. Nobody needs the Brayton cycle.
Say
  • It is a fast-start machine that gives the grid operator a dispatchable option.
  • A jet engine connected to a generator.
Avoid
  • It is a steam plant.
  • Lower efficiency means lower value.

2Why the fleet existsPeaking, contingency, readiness

Fire stations for the grid

Retrieve first. Why would a plant that runs 30 hours a year still matter? Answer in one sentence before reading on.

These plants are not built to run every day. They exist for four jobs: peaking (high demand), contingency (another resource fails), local reliability (imports are constrained), and emergency response (MISO declares an emergency).

A fire station is judged by whether crews and trucks are ready, not by miles driven. Low annual run hours can reflect the intended role. The analogy has a limit: a fire truck emits nothing while idle and costs money to keep ready, while these plants burn gas when they run and the cost of staying available is a real policy question. Name that limit when someone pushes back.

Anchor. Fire station: value is readiness. Limit: these plants emit when they run, and readiness has a cost.
Say
  • Their value is being ready when the grid is tight.
  • They are peaking and contingency generation.
Avoid
  • They only run in emergencies.
  • They guarantee the lights stay on.

3Data disciplineCapacity bases, MW vs MWh, what to label

Numbers need labels

Retrieve first. Which of the three capacity totals would you use in a legislator handout, and what must you print beside it?

MW is power at a moment. MWh is energy over time. Nameplate is the manufacturer rating. Accredited capacity is what the RTO counts for reliability. Capacity factor compares actual output with full-year potential. These are different things and they get confused in the room.

The sources use at least three portfolio capacity totals: 2,092 MW (presentation), 1,918 MW (strategy brief) and higher EIA-style nameplate totals. Use one basis at a time, label it next to the figure, and never add or compare across bases. Legislative districts need address-level GIS verification. A county alone is not enough, because a county can hold more than one district.

Anchor. A pipe and a bucket. MW is pipe size, MWh is water delivered.
Say
  • On the presentation basis, the five plants total 2,092 MW.
  • Run hours are from EPA CAMPD data, 2024.
Avoid
  • About 2 GW (no basis stated).
  • Perry County so it is Senator X (unverified).

4Five plantsFacts per site

Five plants, five different stories

Retrieve first. From memory: which plant runs the most, which has the earliest date, and which pays the most tax?

All five are owned and operated by Ameren Missouri (Union Electric Company) and located in Illinois. They are not identical. Pinckneyville ran 1,836.3 hours in 2024, roughly 21 percent of the hours in a year. The other four each ran under 220 hours. Goose Creek has the largest 2024 property-tax payment. 5EmissionsHonest, fair, specific

Say the emissions part first

Retrieve first. Write one sentence you would say about emissions before a legislator asks.

Natural gas combustion is not zero-emission. Plants emit carbon dioxide and regulated pollutants such as nitrogen oxides when they run. A simple-cycle peaker usually has a higher rate per MWh than an efficient combined-cycle plant. Annual emissions are rate times hours, so a few hours limits the annual total without eliminating the impact.

Stack data does not capture upstream methane. Do not use one unit-level rate or an old year as a fleet-wide statement. The internal IL CTG Matrix mixes confidential and public information, so unit-level emissions, market or compliance details go through Environmental, EM&T, Legal and communications before external use.

Anchor. Honesty is the credibility. Acknowledge, then give context.
Say
  • These plants emit when they run. Low run hours limit the annual total.
  • Annual emissions depend on rate and hours.
Avoid
  • Clean natural gas.
  • Zero-emission backup.

6CEJA and regulationThe policy clock and who decides

CEJA sets the clock, process sets the exceptions

Retrieve first. List the five parts of the retirement readiness test without looking.

CEJA creates emissions endpoints and retirement pressure for fossil units. Internal briefs use January 1, 2030 for Venice (tied to environmental justice and co-pollutant status) and 2040 for the other four. Confirm both against the statute and current IEPA classifications before citing.

A reliability provision lets an RTO determination support temporary continued operation. It is process-based. It is not automatic, not permanent and not a repeal. PJM examples are context, not proof of how MISO will treat a specific Ameren unit. Regulation is layered: EPA and IEPA for air, MISO for dispatch and resource adequacy, FERC for wholesale markets, Missouri regulators for the owner, local bodies for taxes.

The policy test is five questions: is replacement built, interconnected, accredited, deliverable under stress, and is there a credible transition plan for local tax revenue?

Anchor. A traffic light with a process, not an automatic green.
Say
  • A reliability provision is a process tied to an RTO determination.
  • Replacement must be built, interconnected, accredited and deliverable.
Avoid
  • CEJA lets MISO override the law.
  • The exception is permanent.

7Emergencies and communityGrid events and tax-base exposure

When the grid calls, and who is exposed

Retrieve first. Name two things you must confirm before citing the July 15 example.

Demand spikes, a large unit trips, imports are limited, MISO needs reserves. Those are the moments these plants are called. The July 15 example in the source presentation describes more than 17 GW of imports at times, every Ameren-owned gas facility operating, and Venice up to 383 MW. The year is not shown on the slide text. Confirm year, designation, import level and output before citing it.

Internal papers describe managing CEJA rolling limits by entering units into MISO emergency status when 30 hours of allowable run time remain. Treat that as an internal operational description needing Legal, Environmental and Operations approval before external use.

Community: about $2.93 million in 2024 property-tax payments across more than 30 taxing jurisdictions, more than $45.3 million over operating histories, and a ten-year figure above $29.4 million if all five payments disappear using the 2024 base. Present these as tax-base exposure. Schools are the most exposed category. Use salary-equivalent context, never named positions.

Anchor. A hospital generator: you do not judge it by hours, and you do not claim it alone saves the day.
Say
  • They give MISO an additional dispatchable option during tight conditions.
  • These communities have real tax-base exposure.
Avoid
  • The plant prevents all outages.
  • A specific teacher will be laid off.

8Advocacy and scriptsFraming, Q&A, delivery

Balanced frame, plain words

Retrieve first. Answer out loud: a legislator says 30 hours a year proves you do not need the plant. You have 30 seconds.

The strongest argument is not that the plants should run more or stay online forever. It is that retirement timing should be matched to verified replacement capability, deliverability, affordability, environmental compliance and a credible plan for affected communities.

When a skeptic pushes, use four moves: acknowledge the concern, state the verified fact, explain the tradeoff, name the decision test. Avoid framing the issue as reliability versus the environment. Name the actual ask: extension, study trigger, reliability backstop, tax transition, or something else.

Anchor. Acknowledge, fact, tradeoff, test.
Say
  • The question is how to manage the transition so all four goals are met together.
  • Here is the specific request.
Avoid
  • Reliability versus the environment.
  • Taxes decide whether a plant should run.

Plain-language scripts

Read each aloud before you trust it. Spoken time assumes 150 words a minute. Bracketed text means confirm first.

15 seconds · hallway36 words · about 14 sec spoken

Ameren Missouri owns five fast-start gas plants in Illinois. They do not run much, but they are there when the grid is stressed. The question is whether replacements are built and deliverable before the retirement dates.

30 seconds · meeting opener84 words · about 34 sec spoken

Ameren Missouri owns five natural gas combustion-turbine plants in central and southern Illinois. Think of them as fire stations for the grid. They can start quickly when demand spikes, another generator fails, transmission is constrained, or MISO declares an emergency. Most do not run often, and their value is being ready. CEJA sets emissions and retirement deadlines, with Venice first. We support the transition. We are asking that retirement timing match replacement that is built, accredited and deliverable, with a plan for host communities.

2 minutes · briefing174 words · about 70 sec spoken

The five plants are Venice, Goose Creek, Pinckneyville, Raccoon Creek and Kinmundy. They are in Illinois but owned and operated by Ameren Missouri, legally Union Electric Company. Each is a combustion turbine, much like a jet engine connected to a generator.

Their job is not to run around the clock. It is to start fast and give MISO a dispatchable option in tight conditions. Run hours must be read with that in mind. In 2024 Pinckneyville ran far more than the others, and several ran only a few dozen hours. Low use can fit a peaking and contingency role. These plants burn natural gas and emit greenhouse gases and regulated pollutants when they run, so we acknowledge that up front.

CEJA creates the policy clock. Internal materials use January 1, 2030 for Venice and 2040 for the other four. The decision is not fossil versus clean. It is whether replacement is built, interconnected, accredited and deliverable before retirement, and whether host communities have a plan for the property taxes that support schools and services.

Closing line · any length43 words · about 17 sec spoken

The practical question is not whether Illinois should pursue cleaner energy. It is how to manage the transition so reliability, affordability, environmental goals and host-community impacts are addressed together. Retirement dates should be matched to proven replacement capability and a credible transition plan.

Five-minute outline

  1. Start with the machine: a jet engine connected to a generator.
  2. Role: fire stations for the grid.
  3. Name the five sites and the ownership distinction (Illinois sites, Missouri owner).
  4. Run hours: why Pinckneyville is different.
  5. Emissions, honestly and briefly.
  6. CEJA deadlines and the reliability process.

Skeptical-legislator Q&A drill

Open a card only after you have answered it out loud. Compare with the model.

If these plants are so important, why do some run only a few dozen hours?

That is a fair question. The data shows exactly that: in 2024, four of five ran under 220 hours and Pinckneyville ran about 1,836. These are peaking and contingency units. Their value is being available in the hours when supply is tight, like a fire station. The decision test is whether replacement can cover those hours before the plant retires.

Structure: acknowledge · verified fact · tradeoff · decision test. Items in [brackets] need clearance before you say them.

Why change a law built to reduce pollution?

We are not asking to ignore CEJA goals. The plants emit when they run, and we say so. The question is timing and process: retirement should follow replacement that is built, accredited and deliverable. [Insert the specific ask here.]

Can batteries, wind, solar, demand response or transmission replace them?

Over time, many of those can be part of the answer, and that is the point of the test. The question is whether the replacement is operating, accredited by MISO and deliverable to the affected area under stress, not only whether megawatts are installed. Installed megawatts do not automatically replace dispatchable accredited capacity one for one.

Slide deck presentation script guide

A twelve-slide briefing for lobbyists, government relations staff and policy staff new to CTGs. About 48 minutes with the pair practice. Each slide has the on-slide title, the visual, a spoken script, presenter notes, a likely question and a bridge to the next slide.

Design rules
  • One idea per slide. Title states the point.
  • Number labels: basis, year, source on the slide.
  • No dense tables. Move detail to backup.
Delivery rules
  • Ask before telling (slides 2 and 10).
  • Name emissions before you are asked.
  • Say 'at full output' for homes served.
Do not
  • Say a plant alone prevents outages.
  • Cite a district you did not verify.
  • Quote the July 15 event without its year.

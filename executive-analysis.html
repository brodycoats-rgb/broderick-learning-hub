1 · Context

What this plan is, and is not

A Multi-Year Integrated Grid Plan (MYIGP) is a coordinated plan for the electric distribution system, required under the Public Utilities Act as amended by CEJA and refined by CRGA. Filed January 20, 2026; 406 pages; 20 sections.

It is

An engineering roadmap and a regulatory proof document in one.

A single portfolio that links reliability, data, DER, electrification, efficiency, economic development and equity.

A case that must show why, when and at what pace each investment is needed.

It is not

A generation plan, MISO queue study, wholesale capacity plan or transmission plan.

Automatic approval of every project or dollar.

A guarantee that every modeled benefit will occur. The grid plan sets no rates; a rate plan follows in 2027.

3 · Benefits

Who the modeled benefits reach

The filing models about $8.64 billion in total benefits. About 47% is expected to flow to Equity Investment Eligible Communities (EIECs) and low-income customers, above the 40% benchmark the ICC cited in 2023.

$4.08B · 47% EIEC and low-income
$4.56B · other customers
40% benchmark

Reliability and safety

Fewer and shorter outages; safer equipment and operations.

Readiness for growth

Capacity for large loads, EVs, DER and electrification without ad-hoc fixes.

Fairness and access

A tested share of benefits to communities that have been underserved.

Read with care

The 47% is a modeled share of benefits, not bill relief or realized cash savings. Pair it with measurable service and burden outcomes.

5 · The five tests

What makes a grid investment persuasive

Every claim in the plan is judged against five tests. A positive result on one does not excuse a weak answer on another.

Affordability

How much will bills change, for whom, and when?

A governing constraint, not an afterthought

Cost-effectiveness

Do expected benefits justify costs?

Evidence, not a blank check

Equity

Do benefits reach EIECs and low-income customers?

Show it as service people can see

Right-sizing

Is the amount, pace and timing matched to measured risk?

Condition, consequence, probability, timing

Evidence

How strong is the proof behind each number?

Observed data beats announcements

7 · Messaging

How to talk about it

Say“The real test is showing that each piece is needed, timed right and affordable.”

“A modeled share of benefits is not bill relief. Here are the service outcomes we will measure.”

“Growth is welcome, with milestones and protections so other customers are not left holding the risk.”
AvoidImplying cost-effectiveness removes bill impact.

Promising a given investment prevents a specific outage.

Treating economic-development announcements as connected load.

Quoting a total without naming its definition ($2.68B capital additions vs. CUB’s $2.8B).

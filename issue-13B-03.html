Veto session outlook, capacity, RTOs

PJM extended some plants through the state letter process and MISO is more reluctant. Southern Illinois faces property tax and black start worries.

More on Reliability and retirements

What was said

The capacity story started last session. Early in the session the association raised generation capacity concerns with moderate Democrats, using PJM's three year forward looking assessments. The point gained urgency after a Washington based firm dismantled an Illinois power plant and shipped generators to Texas.

The state used a letter process to keep plants available. PJM and the state administration used backstop provisions through a letter writing process. After the session, the Illinois EPA issued a letter saying that regional grid operators have independent authority, under existing state law, to extend the life of plants they need. PJM then extended life allowances across several facilities, including dual fuel units that can burn fuel oil.

MISO is more reluctant. Speaker 1 said thermal generation in the MISO area is mostly coal with few gas units. MISO has been slower to take part in state driven processes and maintains that resource adequacy in vertically integrated markets is a state responsibility.

Southern Illinois would lose tax base. Speaker 1 warned of severe property tax losses for towns and school districts when plants retire. A line about black start was garbled in the notes, but the worry was that the area is down to its last black start resource.

Why it matters

My reading

PJM and MISO are two different tools. The first has leaned in, the second has held back, and Ameren Illinois sits in the second. That is why the Venice and Baldwin conversations turn on what MISO is willing to do. The tax data is a way to make the human cost visible next to the engineering argument.

Say it in plain words

Last session we raised capacity concerns using PJM's own forecasts. After the session, the state environmental agency issued a letter saying grid operators can extend the life of plants they need under existing law. PJM used that, including for dual fuel units. MISO has been more reluctant and says resource adequacy in its kind of market is a state matter. That matters for southern Illinois, which could also lose a lot of local property tax and, in the speaker's words, may be down to its last black start resource. The action is to pull plant by plant tax and economic data.

Background General context I added. It was not said in the meeting.

Two grid operators, two attitudes

PJM runs a competitive capacity market for much of northern Illinois and the East. MISO covers much of the Midwest, including the Ameren Illinois area, where many utilities are vertically integrated and plan their own resources. That structure is why MISO treats adequacy as a state and utility job.

Why tax numbers belong in the argument

Reliability arguments are technical and can be disputed. A school district's lost revenue is concrete and local. Keep the two on separate tracks so each stands on its own evidence.

Terms on this page

Backstop procurement From the meetings
An extra purchase of capacity made when the regular process does not secure enough. A PJM backstop was expected around March and April 2027.
Black start From the meetings
The ability to restart a generator with no power from the outside grid, which helps rebuild the system after a major blackout.
IEPA Background
The Illinois Environmental Protection Agency, part of the plant extension letter process.
RTO Background
Regional transmission organization. PJM and MISO are examples.
PJM Background
The regional grid operator that covers the ComEd area.
MISO Background
The regional grid operator for much of the Midwest, including the Ameren Illinois area.
Dual fuel Background
A plant that can burn more than one fuel, such as gas or fuel oil. Dual fuel units can keep running when one fuel is short.
Property tax base Background
The total taxable value that towns and school districts draw their property tax from. A large plant is often a big part of it.

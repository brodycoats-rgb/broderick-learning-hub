Oct 13

Labor may seek unemployment benefits for striking workers after the election. Today a labor dispute can affect eligibility, so a change could have costs for employers and the unemployment system. One participant thought lawmakers may be more receptive after the election. That is a forecast, not an agreement. Next steps are to watch for a bill, study the eligibility conditions, and compare other states. Opinion

Oct 14

The Bears stadium talks are stuck on tax and money questions. Chicago, Arlington Heights, and Indiana were mentioned as locations. Property tax breaks could reduce money for school districts and other taxing bodies, and education groups were described as opposed where public resources help a stadium. A bill that does not name a site could let lawmakers back a general framework without endorsing a location, but it would still need to cover finances, eligibility, and protections for local governments. Participants contrasted this with the more centralized way Soldier Field was handled. Several approaches are circulating, with no agreement.

Oct 14

Tier 2 pension reform is likely to wait. Participants expected major change to slip past the spring 2027 session unless federal compliance pressure or the Governor forces the issue. Tier 2 generally covers benefit rules for certain public employees hired after January 1, 2011. The federal safe harbor issue involves standards meant to ensure adequate retirement coverage for employees who do not take part in Social Security through their covered job. Changes may eventually be needed, but cost makes them hard. Pension choices also affect budget talks and the attention left for other bills, including energy.

Strike benefits, how unemployment insurance works

The October 13 item was short, so here is the background that makes it easier to follow.

PieceIn plain termsWhy it matters here
Who funds itEmployers pay payroll taxes into a state fund that pays weekly benefits to people who lose work through no fault of their ownA benefit for strikers could raise costs for employers and strain the fund
The usual ruleMany states deny benefits to workers who are out because of a labor disputeThe notes say a dispute can affect eligibility today
What a bill would changeIt could allow benefits after a waiting period, or only in certain casesThe conditions decide the cost
Other statesA few states already pay strikers after a waiting periodThey give a model, and a cost record to study

Background Which states pay strikers, and under what conditions, should be confirmed before you cite them.

Stadium finance, the tools in play

The notes say talks are stuck on tax and money questions. These are the common tools behind that phrase, and who gives up revenue under each.

ToolHow it worksWho gives up revenue
Property tax freeze or breakThe site is taxed at a lower amount for a periodSchools and other local taxing bodies
Payment in lieu of taxesThe owner makes a negotiated payment in place of regular property taxLocal bodies, depending on how the payment is shared
Tax increment financingNew tax revenue from the area is set aside to pay for site costsLocal bodies, for the life of the district
State financing helpThe state backs bonds or pays part of the costThe state budget

Background The notes do not say which of these is on the table. They only say education groups oppose help that comes out of public resources.

Say it in plain words

Three more items. Labor groups may push after the election to let striking workers collect unemployment benefits. Today a labor dispute can affect eligibility, so a change could cost employers and the system, and one participant thought lawmakers might be more open after the vote. That is a forecast. The Bears stadium talks remain stuck on tax breaks that could hit school districts, on financing, and on location. A bill that does not name a site is one idea, but nothing has been agreed. And Tier 2 pension reform is likely to wait unless the federal government forces the issue or the Governor steps in, because the cost is high.

Background

Attention is limited

Pensions, stadium money, and labor bills compete with energy for leadership time and negotiating time. A big pension bill can crowd out other work.

Labor is part of every coalition

Operating engineers, electricians, and other unions showed up in the highway, plug-in solar, and data center talks. Their position can decide whether a bill moves.

Questions to ask
  • If a striker benefits bill is filed, what eligibility conditions does it set?
  • Which stadium approach is actually moving?
  • What would trigger Tier 2 action?
